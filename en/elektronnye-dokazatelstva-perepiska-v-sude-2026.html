Electronic messages as evidence in Russian courts: preservation and authentication

A screenshot can be relevant evidence, but it does not automatically establish who sent a message or whether the exchange is complete. Russian courts assess the source, authenticity, relevance and consistency of electronic correspondence with the rest of the case file.

This is an explanatory translation about Russian law, not advice on foreign law. The Russian version is the primary editorial text.

What Russian procedural law requires

In civil proceedings, Articles 55, 56, 59, 60, 67 and 71 of the Russian Civil Procedure Code govern evidence, burden of proof and judicial assessment. In commercial courts, relevant provisions include Articles 64, 65, 67, 68, 71 and 75 of the Russian Commercial Procedure Code. Neither code makes every screenshot automatically conclusive.

Identify the sender

A contact name saved on a phone is not proof of identity. Link the account to the opposing party through contracts, phone numbers, email addresses, payment records, previous undisputed messages and subsequent conduct. Explain the evidence supporting each identification.

Preserve the complete exchange

Keep the relevant conversation in chronological order, including dates, attachments and context. Retain original files, chat exports and the device where reasonably possible. Avoid cropping messages in a way that changes their meaning. Protect third-party personal information.

Obtain evidence lawfully

Your own correspondence is different from information obtained by unauthorized access to another person's device or account. Do not intercept communications or bypass access controls. Material protected by law may require special handling.

Notarial recording and authenticity disputes

A notary may help preserve evidence where deletion or loss of access is a concern. Notarial recording does not by itself prove who operated an account or whether the statements are true. If authenticity is challenged, the court may consider inspection, additional records or expert analysis.

How to submit the material

State the precise fact each message supports, identify participants and dates, explain how the correspondence was obtained, and attach the relevant printouts and electronic files. If you request inspection of a device, explain why it is necessary. The court decides whether and how to examine the evidence.

Why a screenshot alone is not enough

A message saying “received” does not identify the item received; agreement on a price does not by itself prove performance of a contract; and a recognition of a debt may refer to a different amount or transaction. A lawyer must link every relevant passage to a concrete fact in dispute and corroborate it with contracts, payment records, earlier communications or other evidence. There is no general rule that screenshots are automatically admissible or inadmissible under Russian law.

Practical evidence register and preservation

Build a table with the message date and time, the sender identifier, the verbatim text or file reference, the disputed fact allegedly proved, evidence identifying the account holder and the location of the original. Preserve the complete relevant sequence of messages, audio recordings, attachments, photographs and documents, together with their relationship to the surrounding exchange. Keep exports and original files where possible and preserve the device or account access for potential court examination. Do not conceal material context by cropping or editing screenshots; redact third-party personal information in documents made public only to the extent legally appropriate.

When a notary or technical examination may help

Russian notarial preservation of evidence may be useful when correspondence, a web page or account content is liable to disappear. The notarial record does not automatically identify the person behind an account, prove the truth of the statements or replace the court's assessment. If the opposing party alleges fabrication, identify which messages are challenged and on what grounds. Consider a procedural request for original-device inspection, targeted production of records or a forensic examination only when there are precise issues it may resolve. It would be wrong to promise that every technical expert can establish the actual author from a screenshot alone.

Common evidentiary failures and a pre-hearing checklist

A case can be weakened by an unverified telephone number, selective presentation of convenient phrases, lack of originals, dates inconsistent with bank transfers, disclosure of another person's correspondence without authority or attempts to replace proof of contractual performance with a mere message of agreement. Before the hearing, compare telephone numbers and email addresses with the contract, verify the message period and chronology, connect messages with documents and payments, prepare the required copies for other parties, and plan how to show the original conversation. Do not delete originals after printing; separately decide whether non-parties' data should be restricted from public disclosure.

Sample motion to admit and examine electronic correspondence

To: [court name]
Case No.: [number]
From: [full name or legal entity, procedural status]

MOTION TO ADMIT AND EXAMINE ELECTRONIC CORRESPONDENCE

In support of [identify the specific fact relevant to the claim or defence], I submit correspondence between [participants and their telephone numbers, email addresses or other account identifiers] for the period from [date] to [date]. It is relevant because [explain precisely how the messages relate to the pleaded facts and other exhibits].

The account used by [person] can be identified on the basis of [contract, undisputed earlier correspondence, payment records, other specific corroboration]. The copies were produced in the following manner: [how and when obtained]; the original correspondence is preserved [on specified device, in an account or in export files].

On the basis of Articles 35, 55, 56, 57, 59, 60, 67 and 71 of the Civil Procedure Code of the Russian Federation, I REQUEST:

  1. Admit to the case file the printouts of correspondence on [number] pages and the attached electronic files.
  2. Examine these materials at the hearing and assess them together with the other evidence.
  3. If a reasoned dispute arises as to completeness or authenticity, consider an examination of the original correspondence on [device/account] in a manner that respects the procedural rights of all parties.

Attachments: [list of printouts, exports, media and identity-linking documents]. [Date] [Signature].

For a commercial/arbitrazh court, adapt the legal references and filing method to the Commercial Procedure Code and the actual case. Where the evidence contains third-party personal information or legally protected secrets, assess lawful limits of disclosure.

Relevant Russian law and related materials

Civil Procedure Code of the Russian Federation: Articles 35, 55–57, 59–60, 67 and 71. Commercial (Arbitrazh) Procedure Code: Articles 64–65, 67–68, 71 and 75. The Fundamentals of the Legislation of the Russian Federation on Notaries contain provisions governing preservation of evidence. Verify the current texts and any special provisions applicable on the date of filing.

Practical conclusion

The strongest approach combines the messages with contracts, bank records, correspondence history and a clear explanation of authorship. Keep originals and prepare for challenges before filing.
