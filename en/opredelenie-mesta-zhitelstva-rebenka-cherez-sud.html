Determining a child's residence in Russian courts

Analytical adaptation of the Russian original; individual cases require separate assessment.

Agreement or litigation

Article 65(3) of the Russian Family Code allows parents to agree on residence. If they cannot agree, the court decides in the child's interests, including when parents remain married but live separately.

What the court assesses

Age, attachment to parents and siblings, caregiving history, parental qualities, work schedules and living conditions matter. Under Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 10 of 27 May 1998, better financial or housing conditions alone do not determine the outcome. Article 61 establishes equal parental rights.

The child's views

Article 57 provides a right to be heard. From age ten the child's opinion must be considered unless contrary to their interests. It is not an automatic choice of parent; possible outside influence must be assessed.

Guardianship and interim residence

Article 78 requires participation of the guardianship authority, which examines living conditions and provides a report. The court evaluates it with other evidence. Article 65(3) also permits a request for interim residence pending the final judgment, with mandatory guardianship participation.

Prepare evidence

Collect relevant family records, current residence arrangements, school and caregiving information, work schedules, housing evidence and existing orders. Prepare a factual chronology. Avoid coaching the child or sending sensitive records through unapproved channels.
