Determining a child's residence in a Russian court

This page explains disputes governed by the law of the Russian Federation. A case-specific position requires review of the child's actual routine, family circumstances, evidence and procedural deadlines.

Agreement or litigation

Article 65(3) of the Russian Family Code allows parents living separately to agree where their child will reside. If there is no agreement, the court decides the dispute in the child's interests and takes the child's views into account. The issue can be determined even while the parents remain married.

Record any earlier arrangement, how it operated and what is now disputed. Consider separately whether a schedule for contact with the parent living apart must also be agreed or determined by the court.

What the court assesses

The court considers the child's age and attachment to each parent, siblings and other family members; each parent's actual participation in upbringing; the relationship between the child and each parent; work schedule, family environment and the ability to provide suitable conditions for development. A useful starting point is a concrete description of the child's normal week: school, health care, homework, activities and daily care.

Paragraph 5 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 10 of 27 May 1998 states that better financial or housing conditions alone do not determine the outcome. Evidence must relate to the child's interests rather than merely compare income or floor space.

No automatic preference for the mother or father

Article 61 establishes equality of parental rights and duties. A parent's sex does not create a predetermined advantage. The court assesses the particular family circumstances and the child's interests. A position should therefore focus on real participation in the child's life and the proposed daily arrangements, not general accusations unrelated to the dispute.

The child's views

Article 57 gives the child a right to express views on matters affecting them. From age ten, those views must be taken into account unless this would conflict with the child's interests; a younger child may also be heard where appropriate. The child's statement is assessed with all other evidence and does not amount to an automatic choice. The court may examine whether the view was shaped by pressure from an interested adult.

Parents should not rehearse answers with the child. The method of hearing the child must respect age, emotional state and protection from the conflict.

Guardianship authority

Under Article 78, the guardianship and trusteeship authority must participate in court disputes concerning children's upbringing. It inspects the living conditions and submits an inspection report and opinion. The opinion is evidence, not a substitute for the court's assessment. If it is incomplete, objections should identify specific missing or inaccurate facts and provide supporting material.

Documents and evidence

  • birth, marriage or divorce documents relevant to the dispute;
  • information showing where and with whom the child actually lives and any existing arrangement or court act;
  • housing information, work schedules and a realistic plan for the child's daily routine;
  • lawfully obtained records and correspondence demonstrating participation in care, education and medical decisions;
  • pleadings, inspection reports and guardianship opinions already produced in the case.

Prepare a short chronology and state the requested outcome. Do not collect unrelated private information. The method for transferring documents containing a child's personal data must be agreed separately.

Interim residence while the case is pending

Article 65(3) permits the court, at a parent's request and with mandatory participation of the guardianship authority, to determine where the child will live until the final judgment enters into force. The need for an interim arrangement and its supporting facts are assessed individually; it is not granted automatically.

Residence and contact with the other parent

Residence with one parent does not by itself terminate the other's rights. Article 66 protects the separately living parent's right to communicate, participate in upbringing and take part in educational decisions, unless the form of contact harms the child. If no agreement is possible, the contact schedule may be determined in a separate or connected court dispute.

Frequently asked questions

Must the child stay with the mother?

No automatic preference exists. Both parents have equal rights; the court decides by reference to the particular child's interests.

Does higher income guarantee success?

No. Financial conditions are assessed together with the full circumstances, and the Supreme Court expressly states that a material advantage alone is insufficient.

Does a child over ten decide alone?

The child's view must be considered if consistent with their interests, but the court also evaluates all other relevant circumstances.

Must the marriage be dissolved first?

No. The dispute may be decided when married parents live separately.
