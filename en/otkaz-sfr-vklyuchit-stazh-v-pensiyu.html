Russian pension disputes: missing employment periods

How to challenge the Social Fund’s refusal to recognise employment periods for a Russian insurance pension.

Why employment periods may be missing

Federal Law No. 400-FZ governs insurance periods, including qualifying non-contributory periods. Refusals may arise from illegible stamps, corrections, inconsistent names, missing employer reports, liquidation, incomplete archive certificates or overlapping periods. Employment abroad requires a separate review of the applicable international agreements.

When the Social Fund of Russia (SFR) has refused to recognise a period, obtain the full written decision and the calculation of your insurance service. If you have yet to claim a pension, start by checking your individual account and applying to correct the records.

Prepare a period-by-period record

Obtain your individual account statement through Gosuslugi or the SFR. Compare exact employment dates, employer names, employment book entries, pension coefficients and any entries identifying special working conditions. Check whether qualifying periods such as childcare and military service are recorded where relevant.

For each disputed period, record the reason for exclusion, the documents that support employment and earnings, and the effect on pension eligibility or payment. An additional month may determine eligibility for early retirement. In other cases, correcting a record may increase the length of insurance service without changing the pension start date.

Documents and witness evidence

Before registration in the individual accounting system, employment may be established through employer, public authority and archive records. After registration, individual account information is the primary source. Collect contracts, orders for hiring, transfer and dismissal, payroll accounts, personnel cards, staffing schedules, timesheets, successor records and relevant tax information. Archive certificates should identify the applicant, dates, position, employer and the records on which they are based. Use civil registration documents to explain surname changes and evidence that the records refer to the same person to resolve discrepancies.

If an employer has closed, ask where it transferred its personnel records. Check its former names, any legal successor and the archives of its parent organisation. A negative reply only describes the records held by the archive that answered. Request records of employment, duties and earnings for the exact period at issue and keep written requests and replies.

Where records were lost through no fault of the employee, testimony from two or more former coworkers may establish certain periods under applicable rules. Witness testimony cannot establish the special nature of work for early retirement.

Correction before litigation

Request specific corrections to the individual account through Gosuslugi or an SFR office and attach evidence for each period. A complaint to the higher territorial SFR authority may resolve a refusal without litigation. The right to seek judicial protection remains available. Keep copies of your applications, lists of attachments and acknowledgements of receipt.

When applying for a pension, supplying missing documents within three months of the SFR's explanation can preserve the original application date where the statutory conditions are met. Track the deadline while awaiting archive responses.

Ordinary and special insurance periods

Early retirement may require evidence of full-time employment, occupation, actual duties, production unit, working conditions and interruptions. Compare the position with the applicable historical occupational lists and rules. Obtain clarifying employer certificates, orders, staffing records, technical documents, timesheets and individual account data. If the SFR recognises a period only as ordinary insurance service, ask why it declined to recognise the period for early retirement.

Mistakes that weaken a pension claim

  • Challenging a refusal before obtaining the full written decision and the SFR's calculation.
  • Asking the court to include all years of work without specifying the dates of each disputed period.
  • Submitting an archive certificate that does not identify the underlying records.
  • Leaving surname changes, date discrepancies or corrections to the employment book unexplained.
  • Relying on witnesses to establish hazardous conditions or the special nature of employment.
  • Waiting to apply for a pension when errors in the individual account could be corrected earlier.
  • Failing to calculate how recognition of the disputed period affects the pension start date or the amount payable.

Supreme Court guidance of 26 June 2024

The Supreme Court's review of pension disputes addresses the need to establish actual employment circumstances and apply the rules in force during the relevant period. In one case, the applicant presented an employment book for one period and identified a former employer that might hold records for another. An oral refusal by pension officials to request those records did not remove the need to investigate the facts. The applicant should identify the employer, dates, possible location of records and attempts to obtain them. Ask the SFR to explain how it assessed the evidence and what checks it made.

Three questions to assess a disputed period

  1. Does the evidence establish that you worked during the period?
  2. Do the applicable rules allow the period to count toward ordinary insurance service or early retirement?
  3. Would recognition of the period change the date your pension starts or the amount you receive?

If the SFR points to missing primary records or incomplete account information, assemble a chain of evidence such as an employment order, payroll account, archive reply and records of any company name change. Under Article 57 of the Russian Civil Procedure Code, you can ask the court to obtain a specific record. Explain why it matters and why you cannot obtain it yourself.

Illustrative statement of claim

For an ordinary insurance-period dispute under the Russian Civil Procedure Code, identify the district court, claimant and required identifiers, territorial SFR defendant, any third parties, the value of the claim where applicable and the court fee or basis for exemption. Give the pension application date, the refusal number and date, the date you received it, the exact dates excluded, the employer, your position and the stated reason for refusal. List supporting records, explain discrepancies and prior archive requests, and calculate the effect on your pension rights.

Depending on the facts and the applicable versions of Articles 11, 14 and 22 of Law No. 400-FZ and Articles 56 and 67 of the Russian Civil Procedure Code, the court may be asked to:

  1. Declare unlawful the part of the SFR decision that excludes the specified employment period.
  2. Require the SFR to include that period in insurance service.
  3. Require the SFR to award or recalculate the pension from the lawful date if entitlement and the relevant date are established.
  4. Award documented litigation costs where the law permits.

Attach the refusal, account statement, employment book, name-change records, archive certificates and replies, pension calculation, proof of service on the parties, proof of payment of the court fee or eligibility for exemption, proof of any representative's authority and other supporting evidence. Sign and date the claim.

Check jurisdiction, fees and the entitlement date for the relief you seek. Article 22 ties the commencement date to the application date, when the right arises and any applicable exceptions. A claim seeking only to correct an individual account before the pension is awarded requires different relief. Early retirement, employment abroad and applications to establish a legal fact also require separate assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Can records be corrected before retirement?

Yes. Apply to the SFR to correct your individual account. Checking early gives you time to request employer and archive records before you apply for a pension.

Is an employment book sufficient?

A properly completed employment book is a primary document for periods before individual registration. Discrepancies, corrections and claims involving special working conditions may require further evidence.

Is one witness enough?

The applicable rules require two or more witnesses in specified circumstances involving lost records. Witness testimony cannot establish the special nature of the work.

Do I have to appeal to the SFR before going to court?

A complaint to the higher authority may help correct the calculation without litigation and obtain the SFR's position in writing. The right to seek judicial protection remains available. The appropriate steps depend on the content of the refusal and the deadlines affecting payment.
