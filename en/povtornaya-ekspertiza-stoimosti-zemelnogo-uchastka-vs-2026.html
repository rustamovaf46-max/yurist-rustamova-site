How to obtain a repeat expert valuation of land in a Russian court

Русский · English · 中文 · العربية

When a court valuation relies on unsuitable comparable properties or a defective method, simply saying that the expert is wrong is not enough. Russian Supreme Court ruling No. 81-KG26-6-K8 of 8 September 2026 shows how a reasoned specialist review can support a motion for a repeat expert examination.

Key point: a specialist review does not replace court-appointed expert evidence, but its specific methodological objections cannot be ignored. If the defects concern source data, comparables, methodology or the final value, the court must address a repeat examination and give reasons for refusing it.

The client's problem: the disputed valuation determines the award

In compulsory purchase, damages, property division and other monetary disputes, an expert valuation may determine the amount awarded. An error in the property category, market segment, valuation date or comparables can change the result substantially. The task is to identify a verifiable defect and show how it affected the answer, rather than merely expressing disagreement.

The main intent is how to challenge a court expert's land valuation in Russia. Related questions concern specialist reviews, the difference between additional and repeat examinations, material defects and the content of a motion.

Case No. 81-KG26-6-K8

The owner of agricultural land sought to compel a coal company to purchase the plot after a sanitary protection zone was established. The first expert report valued the land at RUB 39,373,000, and the lower courts relied on that figure.

The defendant filed a specialist review dated 30 May 2025. It identified a specific methodological defect: while valuing agricultural land, the expert used properties intended for subsoil use as comparables. The defendant requested a repeat valuation, but the trial and appellate courts refused.

The Supreme Court set aside the judgments and remitted the case. The reasoned challenge to decisive evidence required the courts to consider a repeat examination. The Court also stressed that inclusion in a sanitary zone did not by itself prove that all agricultural use had become impossible.

The legal test for a repeat examination

Under Article 87(2) of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure, a repeat examination may be ordered when there are doubts about the correctness or reasoning of the first report, or contradictions between experts; it must be assigned to another expert. The report has no predetermined force and is assessed with all evidence under Article 67 and Article 86(3).

A repeat examination re-tests the same questions because the result is doubtful. An additional examination may be appropriate where the report is incomplete or unclear, or new questions arise. Questioning the expert may clarify an answer, but it cannot cure defective source data or methodology.

Constitutional Court decision No. 1308-O of 29 May 2025 does not create an unconditional right to any examination. It protects against an arbitrary refusal where the statutory grounds are substantiated.

Building objections to the report

Compare the court order appointing the examination with the report: were all questions answered and did the expert remain within the assignment? Check the property description, valuation date, land status, permitted use, data sources and comparables.

A useful review identifies reproducible defects: a different market segment, missing adjustments, a wrong date, an arithmetic inconsistency, an ignored restriction or a method unsupported by source data. Link each point to a page of the report and explain its impact.

Ask the court to admit the review, question the expert where useful and order the type of examination actually justified. Questions for the new expert must be neutral and must not assume the desired value.

Evidence and timing

Attach the full report, the appointment order, the specialist review and proof of the reviewer's qualifications, registry extracts, title documents, permitted-use and restriction materials, and verifiable comparable data. For a valuation dispute, identify the legally relevant date and the standards then in force.

File the motion promptly after receiving the report. If the objection arises later, explain when the supporting material became available. Ensure that the hearing record preserves the questions, the motion and the court's reasons for refusal, as these may matter on appeal.

Risks and limits

A specialist review does not automatically invalidate the report or guarantee a repeat examination. The court may find the objections immaterial, resolve an ambiguity by questioning the expert, or rely on other evidence. A second examination adds time and cost, with final allocation usually decided at the end of the case.

Ruling No. 81-KG26-6-K8 did not determine the land's market value and does not promise the same result in another case. Its value lies in requiring a reasoned response where valuation is decisive and specific methodological defects are demonstrated.

Template motion for a repeat examination

To: [name of the Russian court] Case No. [number] Party: [name, address] MOTION FOR A REPEAT COURT-APPOINTED VALUATION By order dated [date], the court appointed an examination. Report [expert/institution, number, date] determined [indicator] as [result]. There are reasoned doubts: page [number] contains [defect]; the expert used [non-comparable data] and omitted [material fact]. This affects the result because [explanation]. The defects are supported by the specialist review dated [date], the reviewer's qualifications and [documents]. Under Articles 35, 67, 79, 86 and 87(2) of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure, I REQUEST that the court: (1) admit the review; (2) appoint a repeat valuation by another expert; (3) put neutral questions concerning value on the legally relevant date; and (4) provide the listed case materials. Attachments: review; qualification documents; property and comparable data; copies for the parties; other required documents. [Date] [Signature] Adapt the motion to the claim and the current Russian procedural rules. If the issue is incompleteness or new questions, an additional rather than repeat examination may be appropriate.

Practical conclusion

A strong motion joins three elements: a precise defect, evidence of that defect, and an explanation of its effect on the legally relevant result. The closer the argument is tied to the report's data and method, the harder it is to dismiss it with a formulaic refusal.
