Challenging an expert report in Russian court: supplementary or repeat examination?

28.11.2026

From an expert report to a procedural objection

A court-appointed expert report is evidence assessed together with the other case materials. Disagreement with its outcome does not automatically require a fresh examination. A useful motion identifies a particular defect and explains why questioning the expert or evaluating other evidence would not resolve it.

Two different procedural remedies

Under Article 87 of Russia's Civil Procedure Code (GPK RF), a supplementary examination addresses an unclear or incomplete report. A repeat examination is relevant when there are justified doubts about correctness or soundness, or contradictions between expert conclusions. Article 87 of the Arbitrazh Procedure Code (APK RF) makes a comparable distinction in commercial proceedings. The same or another expert may conduct a supplementary examination; a repeat examination of the same issues is assigned to another expert.

Identifying a material flaw

Compare the court's order appointing the examination with the final report. Were all questions answered? Which source documents were used? Are methods, measurements and assumptions disclosed? Can the conclusion be traced to the underlying data? Did the expert improperly resolve an issue of law reserved to the judge?

Example: land boundaries

In a boundary dispute, look at the original survey documents, coordinate systems, underlying cadastral data, actual use of the plot and the explanation of any area discrepancy. An unanswered court question may indicate incompleteness; inconsistent measurements or unsupported methods may raise doubts about correctness. The motion should explain which situation applies.

An independent review is not an automatic substitute

A specialist's private review can identify errors and suggest better questions, but it does not by itself guarantee a repeat court examination. A practical evidence matrix records the court's question, the expert's answer, the alleged defect, supporting materials and why the issue matters to the decision.

How to draft the motion

Identify the case and report, quote the exact relevant pages or calculations, state the contradictions and their significance, request the appropriate type of examination and propose questions. Article 79 GPK RF covers parties' procedural rights relating to experts and proposed questions; Article 80 governs the contents of the appointment order. Funding and procedural requirements vary according to the applicable court rules.

If the motion is refused

Ask that the motion and objections be recorded in the hearing record, and use the prescribed procedure for correcting the record when necessary. In an appeal, explain which material facts remained unexamined and why that may have affected the outcome, rather than relying solely on the refusal itself. Check whether a particular order is independently appealable.

Working method

Start with the appointment order, review the source materials and methodology, then articulate a precise evidentiary defect. Seek a supplementary examination for uncertainty or incompleteness; request a repeat examination for substantiated doubts about the report's correctness.
