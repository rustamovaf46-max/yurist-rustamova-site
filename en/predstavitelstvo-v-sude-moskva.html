Litigation lawyer in Moscow | Representation in Russian courts

This is an adapted English overview of Russian litigation services. Russian procedural law applies; the Russian original contains additional detail.

Court representation in Moscow and the Moscow Region

Fatima Rustamova provides legal representation in Russian court proceedings. Assistance may include assessment of a claim, development of a procedural position, drafting statements of claim and objections, preparing motions, analysing documentary and electronic evidence, attending hearings and reviewing grounds for appeal. The scope of an engagement is agreed individually. A favourable result cannot be guaranteed.

Which court hears the dispute?

A case may fall within a court of general jurisdiction or a state commercial (arbitrazh) court. The appropriate forum depends on the parties, subject matter and statutory rules. The Russian Civil Procedure Code and Commercial Procedure Code provide different rules for jurisdiction, evidence, deadlines and appeals. A Russian arbitrazh court is a state commercial court; it is not the same as private international arbitration.

Family and inheritance disputes

Family proceedings can concern property division, child residence, contact arrangements and maintenance. Inheritance disputes can involve acceptance deadlines, proof of actual acceptance, compulsory shares and contested wills. The applicable facts, documents and procedural status must be examined before selecting a claim.

Land, real estate and housing

Land cases may concern boundaries, cadastral registry errors, easements (servitudes), restrictions on land use and refusals by public authorities. Housing cases can concern use of an apartment, eviction, damage caused by flooding and disputes with management organisations. Cadastral extracts, title records, technical evidence and expert reports may be important.

Commercial and other litigation

Commercial cases may concern unpaid supply contracts, corporate disputes, director liability and losses. Other areas include pension decisions, public benefit refusals and certain disputes involving military personnel and their families. The court and legal basis depend on the precise claim.

Evidence, motions and expert examinations

Evidence should be relevant, lawfully obtained and properly presented. The court evaluates evidence as a whole; no lawyer can guarantee that a particular document or expert report will be accepted. Where specialised knowledge is necessary, a party may request a court-appointed examination. The court determines whether to order it, the questions to the expert and procedural cost arrangements.

Before filing and during proceedings

Before filing, check the competent court, limitation or procedural deadlines, mandatory pre-trial steps where applicable, the parties and the evidence. If proceedings have begun, provide the case number, court, date of the next hearing, claims and objections already submitted. Where a judgment exists, supply the full decision and the date it was received so that appeal options can be assessed.

Representation and authority

Authority to represent a party must be documented in the form required by the relevant procedure. In some proceedings a representative must meet statutory professional qualification requirements. The court may require a party's personal participation in certain circumstances. Remote preparation can be discussed, while attendance and formal authority are agreed separately.

Fees, expenses and expectations

Representation starts at RUB 50,000 per court instance. The final fee depends on complexity, documents, hearings and agreed tasks. State duties, experts, notarial services and other external costs may be additional. The successful party may seek recovery of reasonable legal costs subject to procedural rules and judicial assessment; reimbursement is not automatic.

How to request assistance

Briefly describe the type of dispute, court, procedural stage, next hearing or appeal deadline and the outcome you seek. Do not send passport data, information about children or complete confidential case files through public channels before agreeing a suitable transfer method.
