Demolished building, but land rights remained? Russian Supreme Court analysis

Key point: destruction of a building does not always extinguish every right connected with the land at the same moment. In decision No. 18-КГ24-338-К4, included in Supreme Court Review No. 1 (2026), the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation required the lower court to reconstruct the history of the transactions, the law in force at each stage and the basis of land use.

Why the land question is separate

A dispute may involve two different objects: the building and the parcel beneath it. A structure may be demolished or cease to qualify as immovable property, and its register entry may become inaccurate. That fact alone does not prove that a land-use right arising earlier disappeared on the same date. A right to the parcel could have followed the building under the legislation in force when it was acquired and therefore has its own documentary history.

Facts reviewed by the Supreme Court

Paragraph 2 of Review No. 1 (2026) describes a claim challenging transactions with non-residential buildings and seeking removal of their entries from the Unified State Register of Real Estate. According to the case materials, a company sold the buildings in 1995 together with a permanent (unlimited) land-use right created in 1993. The buildings were later resold in 2010 and 2011. In 2021, another participant bought the parcel from the local administration and then challenged the building transactions.

The lower courts granted the claim, referring among other matters to the ruined condition of the structures and the sellers' lack of a proper land title. The Supreme Court set those judgments aside and remitted the case. It did not declare every disputed transaction valid or give either party an unconditional right to the parcel. The trial court still had to determine what rights arose in 1995, when and how the buildings ceased to exist, and whether the land-use right had lawfully terminated.

Error in applying the law

The legality of a 1995 transaction cannot be assessed only under the current Land Code. Article 37 of the Land Code of the RSFSR then regulated transfer of the right to use a parcel together with a building. Special transitional rules could also apply to later disposals and formalisation of land rights.

The Supreme Court also referred to Article 39 of the Russian Land Code in the version effective before 1 March 2015. After destruction of a building, certain rights to the serving parcel could remain if restoration began within the prescribed period. The lower courts had not established the date of final destruction or how the relevant periods ran, so a conclusion on invalidity was premature.

Current regulation

Since 1 September 2022, Article 287.3(4) of the Russian Civil Code has provided that accidental destruction of a building or structure on another person's land does not by itself terminate the land right described by that article, unless a statute or contract provides otherwise; lawful restoration may be possible. This is not a universal rule preserving every land right indefinitely after every demolition. The type and origin of the right, the reason for destruction and public-law restrictions remain material.

Article 235 of the Civil Code concerns termination of ownership when property is destroyed. A party therefore need not defend an inaccurate register entry for a non-existent building at any cost. The issue of that entry must be distinguished from the separate issue of the right to use the parcel.

Decisive evidence

Relevant evidence includes the original act allocating the land, building contracts and annexes, transfer deeds, archival plans and technical passports, the sequence of register entries, inspection reports, photographs, evidence of the cause and date of demolition, restoration applications and correspondence with the administration. A party alleging that the building had already disappeared before a transaction should prove the date and the facts showing that immovable property had ceased to exist. A party alleging termination of land use should identify the legal ground and procedure for termination.

Where physical descriptions conflict, a construction-technical or land-survey expert examination may be necessary. The expert answers technical questions but does not replace the court's legal classification of a transaction.

Arguments from the other side

The claimant may rely on the absence of the building, failure to begin restoration within the applicable period, transfer of the parcel to a third party or missing documents proving the original land right. Each allegation requires a specific answer. Non-existence of the building may support correction of the register but does not necessarily prove that every earlier sale was void. Conversely, a historic land right does not authorise any new construction automatically: permitted use, public restrictions and the basis of allocation must be checked.

The model must be adapted to the evidence and the procedural posture of the particular Russian case.

Procedural strategy

Build a two-column chronology: “building” and “land parcel”. For every transfer, record the date, legal basis, version of the law and supporting document. Then identify the remedy that can actually restore the right: recognition of a land right, invalidity of a transaction, recognition that a right to a destroyed object is absent, or correction of the register. An unsuitable remedy may lead to dismissal even where the underlying facts are persuasive.

2026 update: demolition, flood-zone building restrictions and KRT are different issues

Whether a landowner retains rights after a building is destroyed must be distinguished from whether new construction is lawful. Federal Law No. 301-FZ of 4 August 2026 amended Article 67.1 of the Russian Water Code. Capital construction in flood and waterlogging zones is now prohibited, apart from engineering flood-protection structures. This restriction does not, merely by existing, automatically extinguish ownership of the land.

Nor should the inclusion of a plot in a comprehensive territorial development (KRT) area be treated as a completed expropriation. The dates on which ownership arose, the building ceased to exist, the flood zone was established and the KRT decision was made must be considered separately.
