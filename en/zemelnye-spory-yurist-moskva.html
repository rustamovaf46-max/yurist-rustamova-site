Land disputes in Russian courts

Fatima Rustamova represents parties in land disputes in Moscow and the Moscow Region. The appropriate claim depends on the ownership records, permitted land use, restrictions, existing buildings, cadastral data and conduct of neighbouring owners or public authorities.

Boundary disputes and cadastral errors

Conflicts over parcel boundaries require comparison of EGRN extracts, surveying documents, historic title records and actual use. A registry error is not necessarily the same as a dispute about ownership or the location of a boundary. The claim must address the actual legal defect. Surveying or a court-appointed land expert may be needed.

Easements and access to land

An easement (servitude) may allow limited use of another parcel where statutory requirements are met. The court examines necessity, possible alternative routes, the burden on the neighbouring landowner and proposed terms, including payment where applicable. Mere convenience does not automatically establish entitlement.

Restrictions on land use

Special-use zones may include utility protection zones, sanitary restrictions and aerodrome-related limitations. The effect on a parcel must be checked against official records and applicable rules; the existence of a restriction does not always make all construction impossible.

Refusal to grant or register land rights

Where a public authority refuses to provide land or accept an application, the precise decision, legal basis, the applicant's title to any building and the status of the parcel must be reviewed. A procedural refusal to accept documents differs from a substantive refusal to grant a right. The available judicial route and deadlines depend on the nature of the decision.

Evidence and land expertise

Relevant material can include EGRN extracts, title deeds, land-use regulations, zoning information, survey plans, correspondence and photographs. Expert questions should be tailored to the dispute. The court decides whether to appoint an expert, what questions to ask and how expenses are advanced and allocated.

Stages of representation

Before proceedings, assess the claim, jurisdiction, evidence, limitation and mandatory pre-trial steps where applicable. During proceedings, prepare objections, motions and expert questions. After judgment, examine the decision, applicable appeal deadlines and enforceability.

Fees and first contact

Court representation starts at RUB 50,000 per instance. The final fee, scope and possible third-party costs are agreed in advance. No result is guaranteed. For an initial enquiry, provide the cadastral parcel number without personal information, a short description of the dispute, court and next deadline. Do not send confidential files through open channels.
