Land in a Russian flood zone and KRT redevelopment area: owners' rights in 2026

28 ноября 2026 / 28 Nov 2026

Two separate legal regimes

A parcel may be affected by both a flood or waterlogging zone and a comprehensive territorial development (KRT) decision. These are different regimes. Water legislation addresses permitted activity and flood risk; KRT rules govern potential redevelopment and the procedures affecting property. The existence of one restriction does not establish the legality of the other.

What changed on 4 August 2026

Federal Law No. 301-FZ of 4 August 2026 amended Article 67.1 of Russia's Water Code. A Rosreestr clarification dated 13 November 2026, No. 14-12327-LL/26, explains that construction of capital structures in flood and waterlogging zones is prohibited from 4 August 2026, except for engineering flood-protection structures. For buildings erected or started earlier, the legal analysis requires dates, authorizations and the actual stage of work. The new rule alone does not prove that every existing building must be demolished.

Check the zone itself

Obtain a current extract from Russia's Unified State Register of Real Estate (EGRN), the legal basis of the designated zone, its mapped boundaries and coordinates. Government Resolution No. 360 of 18 April 2014 governs the establishment and revision of these zones. Establish whether the parcel is wholly or partly affected. A claim of boundary error requires technical and documentary evidence, not merely an assertion that flooding has never occurred.

Reconstruct the construction timeline

Gather the purchase documents, recorded permitted land use, planning documents, permits or notifications where required, dated construction photographs, invoices, contracts and surveyor records. Agricultural land requires a separate assessment of the precise statutory basis permitting a home. A foundation or roof does not automatically establish that the structure has lawful completed-building status.

Examine the KRT decision independently

Request the actual decision, boundaries, type of KRT, reasons for inclusion, planning documents and any property-acquisition measures. Articles 65 and 66 of the Russian Town Planning Code govern types of KRT and decision-making. Inclusion in a planned area is not itself a completed expropriation. Where land is acquired for qualifying KRT purposes, special provisions may apply, including Article 56.12 of the Land Code.

Potential remedies depend on the record

Depending on the documents, possible routes include correcting inaccurate register information, contesting an administrative act, challenging the rationale for inclusion, defending pre-existing rights or contesting compensation and procedure if acquisition occurs. The proper defendant, deadline, jurisdiction and form of remedy must be identified before litigation. Starting construction before a law changed is not, by itself, a guarantee that it may be completed.

Evidence checklist

Keep separate bundles for the water zone and the redevelopment decision. The first should include the zone-setting act, coordinates, EGRN records and engineering protection plans. The second should include the KRT decision, permitted-use documents, authorizations, proof of construction stages, expenses and correspondence. A single chronology showing when each right and restriction arose is often decisive.

Conclusion

Assess separately the zone's validity, the August 2026 restriction, pre-existing construction, inclusion in KRT and any acquisition decision. Otherwise a lawsuit may be brought on an incorrect factual or legal basis.
