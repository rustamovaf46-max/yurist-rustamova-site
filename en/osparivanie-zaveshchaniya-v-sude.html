Challenging a will in Russia: grounds, evidence and expert examination

Русский · English · 中文 · العربية

By Fatima Rustamova, legal specialist and court representative. Original published 21 October 2026.

An heir may learn from the notary that an apartment or other property was left to someone else. Concerns sometimes arise because the testator was seriously ill, relied on others for assistance, behaved unusually, or appears not to have signed the will. Disagreement with the distribution does not invalidate a will. To challenge it, identify a legal ground for invalidity and prove what was happening when the will was made.

A will can be challenged after the inheritance has opened. Keep track of the separate six-month period for accepting the inheritance. Filing a challenge does not take the place of an application to the notary or automatically preserve that deadline.

Who can challenge a will, and when?

Under Article 1131 of the Russian Civil Code, a person whose rights or legitimate interests are affected by a will may bring a claim. This is typically someone who would inherit under the law or another valid will if the document being challenged were invalid. An ordinary will cannot be challenged while the testator is alive. The inheritance has yet to open, and the testator can still change or revoke the will.

Start by checking which will is the most recent, whether it has been revoked, who has already applied to the notary, and what assets are in the estate. A relative's report that a new will exists is insufficient to identify the right claims and evidence.

Grounds that can make a will invalid

The testator could not understand the meaning of their actions

Article 177 of the Russian Civil Code provides a common ground. Even a person with full legal capacity may, at a particular moment, be unable to understand the meaning of their actions or control them. The court examines the testator's condition at the date and time the will was made. A diagnosis, advanced age or the fact of receiving treatment needs to be assessed in relation to that moment.

Relevant evidence may include medical records, prescriptions, ambulance records, hospital admissions, specialist care and the testator's behaviour before and after visiting the notary. The court looks at how close that information is to the date of the will and what it shows about the testator's ability to express their wishes knowingly.

Defects in form or authentication

Articles 1118, 1124 and 1131 of the Russian Civil Code set requirements for personal execution, the testator's legal capacity, written form and authentication, as well as witnesses where the law requires them. A material breach may make the will void or provide grounds for a court to declare it invalid.

A typo, correction or an inaccurate reference to the place or time does not automatically invalidate a will. Article 1131(3) of the Civil Code and paragraph 27 of Russian Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 require the court to determine whether the defect affected how the testator's intentions could be understood.

Someone else signed or the testator's wishes were distorted

If the authenticity of the signature is in dispute, the parties may need the original will, handwriting samples made independently of the proceedings, and a forensic handwriting examination. Allegations of deception, violence, threats or other influence require facts relevant to the particular ground of invalidity. Suspicions that an heir influenced the testator are insufficient on their own.

What is insufficient on its own?

  • Advanced age, a disability, serious physical illness, or taking medication without evidence of an effect on the ability to understand one's actions.
  • An allocation of property the relatives consider unfair, or the testator having no contact with a beneficiary named in the will.
  • A sudden change of mind without proof of a legal ground of invalidity.
  • General impressions from witnesses such as “they confused the dates,” “they looked weak” or “they seemed fine,” without a specific account of events and comparison with other evidence.
  • Authentication of the will at home or in a medical facility if the applicable legal requirements were met.

Which evidence should you gather first?

  1. Probate file. Keep a record of your application to the notary and collect information about the heirs and the will in dispute. If you cannot obtain a document yourself, explain in the claim why the court should order its production.
  2. Medical records. Relevant records may include outpatient and inpatient notes, ambulance call records, tests, prescriptions and discharge summaries from around the time the will was made. The court can consider material subject to medical confidentiality through the procedure prescribed by law.
  3. Information from the notary. The circumstances of authentication, the notary's conversation with the testator, who was present, and why someone signed at the testator's request may all be relevant. The notary's professional assessment is considered with the whole file. Medical questions may require expert evidence.
  4. Witness testimony. Look for specific observations. Did the testator recognise people, understand events and discuss their property independently? What did they do during the period in question?
  5. Property records. Find out which assets are in the estate and what has been registered. If assets may be transferred, assess the need for interim measures and whether those measures would be proportionate.

Under Article 56 of the Russian Civil Procedure Code, each party must prove the facts on which it relies. A claimant should therefore support their claim with evidence. Asking the defendant to prove that the testator understood their actions is usually insufficient.

How a posthumous forensic psychiatric examination works

When a dispute requires specialist knowledge, the court appoints an expert under Article 79 of the Russian Civil Procedure Code. In a posthumous examination, the expert reviews medical records, information about the person's behaviour and other material in the case file. A fuller, more precise record reduces the chance that the expert cannot reach a conclusion because of gaps in the evidence.

Experts are usually asked whether the testator had a mental disorder or another condition and whether, when signing the will, they could understand the meaning of their actions and control them. The court settles the questions. The parties can propose wording and explain which material the experts should receive.

In ruling No. 24-КГ14-7 of 2 December 2014, the Russian Supreme Court explained why specialist knowledge matters. Witnesses can describe actions and behaviour, while assessing the person's mental condition requires professional expertise. The court evaluates the expert's report along with the other evidence and must give reasons grounded in the full record if it disagrees with the report.

Limitation periods and the six-month inheritance deadline

For a voidable transaction, Article 181(2) of the Russian Civil Code generally provides a one-year limitation period starting when the claimant learned or should have learned of the grounds for invalidity. Claims concerning void transactions are subject to a three-year period under Article 181(1), with separate rules on when the period starts. For a person who was not a party to the transaction, there is a ten-year upper limit.

Identify whether the transaction is voidable or void before calculating the limitation period. Then establish when performance began and when the particular claimant learned the relevant facts. The defendant must invoke the limitation period. It is risky to assume they will remain silent.

Article 1154 of the Civil Code governs acceptance of the inheritance separately. The general rule is six months from the opening of the inheritance. If you consider yourself entitled to inherit if the will is held invalid, record that position with the notary in time. If the period has already expired, assess the available options separately, including whether the deadline to accept the inheritance can be restored.

Preparing the claim and identifying the parties

Connect the facts to a specific legal ground in the statement of claim. Identify the will and the rights it affects, describe how the testator's inability to understand or control their actions arose or which form requirement was breached, specify the evidence, and state what legal consequences of invalidity you seek.

The proper defendants and third parties depend on who acquired rights under the will, whether inheritance certificates were issued, whether property was registered, and the relief sought. The notary does not automatically become a proper defendant because they authenticated the document. The requested relief and assets also affect jurisdiction and the court fee.

If the will is declared invalid, the estate does not automatically pass in full to the claimant. The general rules of succession or another valid will apply, taking account of the heirs, mandatory shares, acceptance of the inheritance and the other circumstances.

Common mistakes when challenging a will

  • Waiting for another heir's certificate while letting your own deadline to accept the inheritance pass.
  • Stopping after a medical provider declines to release confidential records, without asking the court to order their production.
  • Relying only on witnesses without checking for medical files or other objective records from around the date of the will.
  • Asking an expert whether the will is legally valid instead of posing questions that require the expert's specialist knowledge.
  • Challenging a superseded will or omitting the relief needed to restore inheritance rights.

Frequently asked questions

Is psychiatric treatment alone enough to challenge a will?

That fact alone is insufficient. A diagnosis is relevant, but the court establishes the testator's condition when the will was made. Relevant records, a clear chronology and, where necessary, a court-appointed examination help establish that condition.

Am I bound to lose if the notary says the testator seemed fine?

The notary's account matters and is assessed with the medical records, expert opinion and other evidence. The outcome depends on the full record.

Can I obtain an expert opinion before filing?

A specialist can carry out an initial review of the documents. The court appoints a forensic examination under Article 79 of the Russian Civil Procedure Code and decides what material and questions go to the expert. An out-of-court review may still be followed by a court-appointed examination.
