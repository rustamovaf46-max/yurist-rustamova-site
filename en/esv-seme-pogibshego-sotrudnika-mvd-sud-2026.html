Housing benefit for the family of a deceased Russian police officer

This is a full English-language editorial analysis of item 9 of the Russian Supreme Court Review of Judicial Practice No. 1 (2026), determination No. 60-KG25-1-K9. The author does not claim to have represented the parties. The sample claim below must be adapted to the facts and procedural rules of a particular case.

Why selling a home does not always disqualify a family

A family applying for a one-time social housing payment (ESV) may be refused registration because a house or apartment was sold within the previous five years. Yet the interval between the sale and the application cannot, by itself, establish the decisive legal element: an intention to worsen housing conditions artificially in order to qualify for the benefit. The real reasons for relocation, the suitability of the former home, the family's composition, expenditure of the sale proceeds and the statutory eligibility conditions all require examination.

Facts of the Supreme Court case

Item 9 of Review No. 1 (2026) discusses the family of a police officer who had served since 2010 and was transferred to the Kamchatka region in 2017. In 2018 the family sold a 113-square-metre house in the Rostov region. The officer died in 2022 from an illness contracted during service. His widow then applied to be entered on the register for a one-time housing payment, with a five-member family. The internal-affairs commission refused because fewer than five years had elapsed between the sale and the officer's death and application.

The widow explained that the sale followed the transfer and the move; the proceeds went towards a mortgage and consumer loan used to support the family. At the relevant time the family had no home in ownership and rented accommodation commercially. The trial court investigated the transfer, finances, use of proceeds and living conditions and found the refusal unlawful. The appellate and cassation courts instead treated the recent sale itself as sufficient grounds for refusal.

The Supreme Court's reasoning

By determination No. 60-KG25-1-K9, the Civil Chamber of the Russian Supreme Court set aside the appellate and cassation rulings and upheld the trial court. The central rule is Article 4(7) of Federal Law No. 247-FZ of 19 July 2011: the five-year restriction concerns intentional acts that worsen a claimant's housing conditions in order to obtain registration for the social benefit. Disposal of housing or a share is one of the listed acts, but listing a transaction does not prove the necessary motive on its own.

The review must consider the life circumstances that prompted the sale. The Court's approach should be read together with Article 53 of the Russian Housing Code and the rules on ESV approved by Government Decree No. 1223 of 30 December 2011. An adverse decision based merely on the date of sale, without assessing intention and the evidence, is insufficiently reasoned.

Eligibility and evidence the family must establish

First verify the statutory basis for the benefit: the deceased employee's status and length of service, the cause of death, family composition, any required cohabitation, whether a previous subsidy was received, and the family's genuine housing need. In the reviewed case relevant factors included more than ten years' service and the absence of an earlier payment, owned accommodation or a social tenancy. Not every relative of every deceased employee is automatically eligible; the general and special conditions of Law No. 247-FZ must each be checked.

For the transaction itself collect the transfer order, records of relocation, the sale agreement, bank statements, proof of mortgage or loan repayment, tenancy agreement after the move, registration at the new address and EGRN extracts. A clear, documented causal connection between the work-related move, the sale and the family's real needs is stronger than a general assertion of good faith.

How to frame the court proceedings

Obtain the commission's written refusal with its date and legal reasons, the commission minutes, the registration file, any calculation of housing area per family member, records of accommodation held by relatives and the authority's supporting requests. Check the appropriate court, procedural form, jurisdiction and filing deadlines under the applicable rules. The dispute in the Supreme Court review concerned registration for ESV, not an order for immediate transfer of money to the widow. These are distinct remedies.

The likely authority argument is that the statute explicitly lists sale as a deterioration of housing conditions. The answer should address Article 4(7) as a whole: the transaction and the claimant's intention must be evaluated together, using proof of the circumstances. Citation of the Supreme Court ruling alone is no substitute for evidence specific to the claimant's family.

Adaptable sample statement of claim

To: [name of district court, subject to applicable jurisdiction]
Claimant: [full name, address, contact]
Respondent: [MVD unit or authorised body, address]
Other parties, if applicable: [details]

STATEMENT OF CLAIM seeking recognition of an unlawful refusal to register for a one-time social housing payment and an order to place the claimant on the register.

My spouse [name, service details] served in the internal-affairs bodies from [date] and was transferred to [new station] on [date]. He died on [date] due to [cause supported by medical and service records]. Our family consists of [members, including children and relevant details].

On [date] the commission of [body] issued refusal No. [number] to register us for ESV. The reason given was sale of accommodation at [address] on [date], less than five years previously.

The sale was caused by [the documented service transfer, relocation, necessity of debt repayment or other particular circumstances]. The proceeds were used for [documented uses]. There was no intention to create artificial housing need to obtain ESV. Current housing conditions are [area, ownership or tenancy, evidence]. The legal preconditions for registration are established by [service records, death and family documents, EGRN extracts and other evidence].

Under Article 4(7) of Federal Law No. 247-FZ, intentional deterioration is material. Item 9 of Supreme Court Review No. 1 (2026), determination No. 60-KG25-1-K9, requires examination of the motives and circumstances of disposal rather than automatic disqualification on the basis of sale.

I REQUEST THAT THE COURT:

  1. Declare unlawful the decision of [body, date and number] refusing registration for ESV.
  2. Order [body] to register [eligible person and family members] where the legal requirements have been established, or otherwise remedy the proven infringement by the relief appropriate to the dispute.
  3. Require production of the registration file, commission minutes and underlying documents if the claimant could not obtain them independently, stating the reasons.
  4. Decide recoverable litigation costs where legally justified.

Attachments: refusal, marriage and birth certificates, death and service records, EGRN extracts, sale agreement, transfer order, evidence of the move and use of the proceeds, accommodation documents, proof of service on other parties and applicable court-fee evidence or exemption. [Date] [Signature].

This example is not a filed claim; the precise form, court, parties and relief require legal review.

Limits of the ruling

The decision concerns ESV under Law No. 247-FZ for the family of an internal-affairs employee who meets the specific statutory criteria, not every housing subsidy or all public-sector employees. Proven necessity for the sale may be decisive, but other facts may produce a different outcome. An obligation to register does not mean an immediate cash payment or a guaranteed amount. The claim must be adapted to the claimant's legal status and the correct form of civil proceedings.
