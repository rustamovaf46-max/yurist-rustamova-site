Salary overpayment in Russia: when may an employer recover it? Supreme Court 2026

Key point: an overpaid salary is not automatically recoverable. In Review No. 1 (2026), the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation explained when an incorrect number of shifts entered into payroll software may amount to a calculation error.

Why the dispute arises

An employer discovers that substantially more money was transferred than was due. The employee understood the payment to be salary, while the organisation demands repayment. The answer depends on the cause of the overpayment, the calculation mechanism, the recipient's conduct and the evidence—not on the overpayment alone.

What the Supreme Court considered

Paragraph 8 of Supreme Court Review No. 1 (2026) concerned payment for 10.64 shifts. Instead of 10.64, payroll software received 1,064 shifts. The employee was paid RUB 1,814,880.35 instead of RUB 10,570.98; the disputed difference was RUB 1,804,309.37. The first-instance and appellate courts upheld the employer's claim. The cassation court characterised the mistake as merely technical and set those judgments aside. The Supreme Court found that distinction unjustified, quashed the cassation ruling and left the recovery judgments in force. The decision is No. 46-КГ25-8-К6.

The legal boundary

Article 137(4) of the Russian Labour Code provides a closed list of exceptions to the general prohibition on recovering excess salary: a calculation error, certain cases of employee fault established by the competent body, and unlawful conduct established by a court. Article 1109(3) of the Civil Code protects means of subsistence when there is neither bad faith nor a calculation error.

The Supreme Court explained that an erroneous number of worked periods entered into software and affecting an arithmetic result may be a calculation error. This does not make every payroll or accounting mistake recoverable. A duplicate transfer, an incorrect interpretation of law and an arithmetic miscalculation require different proof. The Review also refers to Constitutional Court Resolution No. 1-П of 11 January 2022.

What the employer must prove

The employer should produce the employment contract and pay rules, time sheets, payslips, original payroll inputs, input and correction logs, payment records, explanations from responsible staff and a transparent calculation of the claimed difference. A managerial order stating that a “calculation error” occurred does not prove its mechanism. The evidence should show which figure was entered, what it should have been and how it changed the arithmetic result.

How the employee may respond

Request payslips, time sheets, the applicable remuneration rules and the source data underlying the demand. Check whether a duplicate transfer, a retrospective change to pay conditions or an incorrect legal interpretation is being presented as a calculation error. Bad faith must also be proved: receipt of money alone does not always establish it. At the same time, a sudden many-fold increase over ordinary pay and the employee's subsequent conduct may be assessed together by the court.

Model procedural motion for disclosure of evidence

To [name of court], case No. [number].
Claimant: [employer]. Defendant: [name].

MOTION FOR DISCLOSURE OF EVIDENCE

The court is hearing a claim to recover RUB [amount] as salary allegedly paid in excess. The claimant relies on a calculation error. Under Article 57 of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure, I ask the court to order the claimant to produce:
1. time sheets for [period];
2. payslips and a detailed calculation;
3. documents establishing rates and the calculation method;
4. an export of the original payroll-software inputs and records of changes;
5. documents confirming the actual transfers.

I could not obtain these records independently because [describe request and reason]. They are necessary to distinguish an arithmetic calculation error from other causes of overpayment.

[Date] [signature]. Attachments: copy of request to employer and proof of sending.

The wording must be adapted to the actual case and Russian procedural requirements.

Practical conclusion

For a claim or defence, reconstructing the payroll formula is more useful than debating the label “technical”. In the reviewed case, the decisive point was how the wrong number of shifts affected the arithmetic calculation. The Supreme Court's conclusion remains tied to the established facts and does not remove the restrictions of Article 137 for other situations.
