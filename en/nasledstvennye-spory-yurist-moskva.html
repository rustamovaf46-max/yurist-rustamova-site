Inheritance litigation under Russian law

Inheritance disputes may concern the identification of heirs, acceptance of an estate, property not registered in the deceased person's name, compulsory inheritance shares, challenges to wills and the division of inherited property. The applicable rules are primarily found in Part Three of the Civil Code of the Russian Federation and the relevant procedural legislation.

Acceptance of inheritance and deadlines

As a general rule, inheritance must be accepted within six months of the opening of the estate. Acceptance may be formalised through a notary or established through conduct showing actual acceptance, depending on the facts. A missed deadline is not automatically restored: the court examines statutory grounds, evidence and timing. In certain circumstances, heirs can resolve late acceptance without litigation if the legal requirements are met.

Actual acceptance of an estate

Relevant evidence may include possession and maintenance of inherited property, payment of related expenses, protection of the estate and management of its assets. Not every payment or family relationship is sufficient by itself. The evidence must be linked to the legal requirements and the applicable period.

Unregistered property and ownership shares

Where the deceased person's title was not registered, the legal basis for ownership and the appropriate procedural claim must be examined. An extract from the Unified State Register of Real Estate (EGRN), earlier title documents, notarial materials and evidence of possession may be relevant. The absence of a registry entry does not by itself resolve whether the property forms part of the estate.

Challenging a will and compulsory shares

A will may be challenged only on legally recognised grounds, supported by admissible evidence. Disagreement with its contents is not enough. Certain persons may have a right to a compulsory share despite the will; eligibility and calculation depend on the circumstances and applicable provisions of the Civil Code.

Preparing the claim and evidence

Collect the death certificate, documents establishing family relationship or another basis for inheritance, the will if any, notarial records, title documents, EGRN extracts and evidence concerning acceptance and possession. Identify other heirs and interested parties, the notary and the appropriate court. Time limits, jurisdiction and the formulation of claims require individual analysis.

Choosing the correct remedy and assessing deadlines

Not every notarial refusal requires litigation, and failure to apply to a notary does not necessarily mean the estate was not accepted. Under Article 1155 of the Russian Civil Code, judicial restoration requires statutory grounds, including a valid reason for missing the deadline and application to court within six months after that reason ceased. Merely saying that the heir did not know a notarial application was necessary is often insufficient. Distinguish restoration from recognition of actual acceptance. Joint residence, possession, maintenance, utility payments, repayment of the deceased's debts and protection of assets may be relevant; establish when these acts occurred, the intention to accept and objections of other heirs. A dispute over rights generally requires contentious claim proceedings rather than merely establishing a legal fact.

For unregistered property, establish whether the deceased owned it when succession opened. Inclusion in the estate and recognition of ownership are distinct remedies. When challenging a will, examine its execution and certification, the testator's condition at the legally relevant date, medical evidence and possible posthumous forensic examination. Identify eligible compulsory-share heirs under Article 1149 and calculate their shares against the estate composition.

Frequently asked inheritance questions

Is paying apartment bills enough?

It may support actual acceptance but does not guarantee recognition of ownership. Timing, purpose, documents and the overall circumstances matter.

What if the deadline expired years ago?

Distinguish actual acceptance from restoration under Article 1155, including the separate six-month period after the valid reason ceased.

Can dissatisfaction invalidate a will?

No. A legally recognised defect and admissible evidence are required.

Must a claim be filed immediately?

Not always. A notarial solution may be available where acceptance is documented or other heirs consent under statutory conditions.
