Being denied access to your apartment in Russia: when a court can order admission

Русский · English · 中文 · العربية

When a person is locked out of a home, the first question is why they have the right to live there. Ownership, co-ownership, a family member's right of use and social tenancy are different legal foundations. A dispute between relatives or a changed lock does not by itself establish the claimant's legal entitlement; the court must establish both the right and a specific unlawful obstruction.

Who may bring a claim?

An owner has rights of possession and use under Article 30 of the Russian Housing Code, subject to statutory limits. A co-owner's entitlement must also be assessed under Article 247 of the Civil Code, which governs agreement or a court determination of the use of shared property. For family members of an owner, Article 31 of the Housing Code requires examination of the circumstances of moving in, the owner's intention, family relations and agreements. After the family relationship ends, the right of residence may change; a former family member cannot simply be treated as an owner.

What is evidence of obstruction?

Relevant conduct includes preventing entry, changing the locks, refusing to provide keys, physically blocking access or repeatedly denying admission. The claim should state when and by whom these actions occurred. An EGRN extract or other title document, messages requesting admission, responses, witness testimony, legally obtained recordings, and records of genuine complaints to authorities can help. For co-owners, add the apartment plan, ownership shares and any actual agreement on use. Do not rely on unlawful forced entry to improve the evidential position.

Admission and allocation of rooms are different claims

A claim for admission and removal of obstructions protects access and use. A claim to determine the order of use addresses which rooms and shared facilities co-owners may use. A person may have the right to enter but not necessarily to demand the exclusive use of a particular room. Combining claims may be appropriate in some disputes, but should follow the actual facts, floor plan, existing arrangements and the interests of other co-owners.

The three-part test

A persuasive case connects (1) the legal basis for using the apartment, (2) documented interference caused or maintained by the defendant, and (3) a sufficiently precise and enforceable order. Ownership documents alone do not prove who blocks entry. Conversely, proof of a changed lock does not confer a right of residence on someone who no longer has one. Under Article 304 of the Civil Code, an owner may claim removal of interference not linked to dispossession where the statutory conditions are satisfied.

Supreme Court guidance on registration and residence

Resolution No. 14 of the Plenum of the Supreme Court of Russia, 2 July 2009, explains that registration at a residential address, or the absence of registration, is not conclusive of family-member status. Paragraph 11 directs courts to examine the circumstances and purpose of moving in, the owner's intention and the nature of the relationship. The protection against arbitrary deprivation of housing does not mean every requested order for admission must be granted. A court must establish the person's actual legal status and the proven interference.

Defendant's objections

The argument that the claimant has not lived in the flat for a long time does not terminate registered ownership. For tenants or family members, however, whether departure was voluntary, whether possessions remained, and whether the legal basis of use ended may matter. Having another flat does not itself erase ownership of the disputed apartment, although it may be relevant in some types of housing claim. The absence of a spare room may matter to room allocation but does not automatically resolve an access claim.

How to prepare the proceedings

Establish ownership or another valid residence right, identify the person preventing access, document the dates and conduct, select claims that can be enforced, and assemble documents required by Articles 131–132 of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure. An order for keys should identify the relevant door and why the defendant can provide them. Costs, service of documents, representation authority and any need for an expert or a witness should be assessed before filing.

Illustrative claim for an owner or co-owner

TO [district court, jurisdiction checked according to the particular relief]. CLAIMANT: [full name, address, birth details and required identifier]. DEFENDANT: [available identification and address]. Property: [address and cadastral number]. Court fee: [calculate under the current version of Article 333.19 of the Russian Tax Code]. STATEMENT OF CLAIM: I hold [sole ownership / stated share] in the apartment at [address], evidenced by [EGRN extract/title]. Since [date] the defendant has [specific acts: changed locks, refused access, withheld keys]; requests for admission on [dates] were unsuccessful, supported by [communications and other evidence]. In accordance with Civil Code Articles 209, 247 where relevant and 304, Housing Code Article 30, and Civil Procedure Code Articles 3 and 131–132, I REQUEST: (1) order the defendant not to obstruct my use of the apartment at [address]; (2) order my admission where the facts and relief require it; (3) order delivery of a set of keys for [the specified entry door], if justified and enforceable; (4) award proved litigation costs. Attach title documents, evidence of refusal, correspondence, applicable fee receipt or exemption, proof of sending proceedings to other participants, and authority documents where relevant. [Date, signature].

Jurisdiction and limits of the sample

Housing admission claims are usually heard at first instance by district courts. Territorial jurisdiction must be determined by the particular claim: Article 30 of the Civil Procedure Code applies to disputes concerning rights to immovable property, but not every complaint of obstruction is automatically such a dispute. A family member, social tenant, former spouse or person whose rights have terminated needs a different legal basis and possibly different relief. Check the current court fee and the identities and addresses of the parties.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sue if I am refused keys? Potentially yes, if you have a subsisting right to use the home and can prove the refusal. Is permanent registration enough? No: it is not the same as ownership or an independent residence right. What changes after divorce? Article 31 of the Housing Code, possible agreements and the particular history must be considered. Must the court allocate me a room? Not necessarily; admission and the order of use are different matters. Is admission guaranteed? No. The evidence and legally sound choice of remedy determine the prospects.
