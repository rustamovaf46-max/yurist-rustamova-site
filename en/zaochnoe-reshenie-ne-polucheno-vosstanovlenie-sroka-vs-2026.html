Default judgment not received: how to restore the time limit

If a Russian court decided a civil case in the defendant's absence and the mailed judgment was never actually delivered, the label “storage period expired” does not automatically shift every consequence to the addressee. Supreme Court determination No. 24-KG26-8-K4 of 10 November 2026 shows which delivery facts must be examined and how an application should be structured.

Core holding: a legally significant communication may be treated as delivered where non-receipt was caused by the addressee. That rule cannot restrict access to justice where the letter was not received for objective reasons. The court must examine the address, delivery attempts, tracking events and other evidence rather than rely solely on the returned envelope.

The practical problem

A default judgment may already have led to enforcement or debits before the defendant learns of the litigation. A bare statement that “I did not see the letter” is weak. The applicant should reconstruct a verifiable timeline: the address used by the court, the arrival of the item at the post office, any attempted delivery, the notice sent to the addressee, the actual date on which a copy was obtained, and the procedural action taken immediately afterwards.

The principal issue is restoring the time limit for applying to set aside a default judgment when judicial mail was not delivered. Related questions include proof of non-receipt, the effect of an incorrect address, the meaning of a return after the storage period, the correct court, and the need to file the substantive set-aside application at the same time.

Supreme Court case No. 24-KG26-8-K4

By a default judgment of 11 December 2024, a district court awarded RUB 755,105.01 in road-accident damages against the defendant. On 22 July 2025 he applied to set the judgment aside and requested restoration of time, stating that he obtained the judgment only on 16 July. The first-instance, appellate and cassation courts refused because a letter had been sent to his registered address and returned after expiry of the postal storage period.

The Civil Chamber of the Supreme Court looked beyond that final postal status. Tracking recorded arrival on 14 January 2025 and return on 25 January but no delivery or attempted delivery. The envelope used house 32B instead of house 32. A local-government certificate also recorded objective restrictions on postal delivery. The Supreme Court set aside the three rulings and restored the time limit itself.

Why an expired storage period is not always avoidance

Article 165.1(1) of the Russian Civil Code and paragraphs 67–68 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 25 of 23 June 2015 allow a communication to be regarded as delivered if it reached the addressee but was not received for reasons attributable to that person, including avoidance of collection. The attributable reason must first be established. A wrong address, no recorded attempt to deliver, or an objective disruption is not the same as avoidance.

The Supreme Court connected that inquiry with Article 46(1) of the Russian Constitution and Article 112 of the Civil Procedure Code. Restoration is not an arbitrary favour; it is a mechanism for balancing finality with effective access to justice. The ruling does not excuse every returned letter. The applicant must provide specific evidence that the cause was independent of them.

Time limits and procedural sequence

The determination applied Article 237(1) of the Civil Procedure Code, under which the defendant could apply within seven days after service of the judgment copy. The Code in force on the legally relevant date and the precise posture of the case must always be checked. Under Article 112, the omitted procedural act must be performed together with the restoration request. Ordinarily the filing therefore includes both the request to restore time and a substantive application to set aside the default judgment.

Setting aside the judgment remains a separate issue. The applicant should explain the justified absence and identify facts and evidence capable of affecting the outcome. Restoring time alone neither guarantees that the judgment will be set aside nor determines the merits.

Evidence to collect

  • the complete envelope showing the address, barcode and postal marks;
  • a detailed tracking report with every event and any delivery attempt;
  • a postal, municipal or other official certificate of an objective delivery problem;
  • proof of the correct address and of the sender's error;
  • proof of the actual date on which the judgment was obtained and prompt filing thereafter;
  • defences and documents that could affect the original claim.

A tracking screenshot is stronger when supported by an official response. Publication on a court website may be relevant, but it does not itself correct a wrong postal address or prove service of a specific letter on an individual.

Counterarguments and limits

The opposing party may show that the defendant supplied the wrong address, moved without notifying the court, received the initial notice, refused the letter, or delayed after actual receipt. Those facts may change the assessment. A submission should account for each date rather than overstate one document.

Failure to receive notice of the hearing, failure to receive the judgment, and missing the deadline for a procedural act are related but distinct. The requested relief must match the actual stage of the Russian proceedings.

Sample application

To: [court that issued the default judgment]
Case No.: [number]
Claimant: [name/address]
Defendant: [name/address/telephone]

APPLICATION TO RESTORE THE TIME LIMIT AND SET ASIDE A DEFAULT JUDGMENT

On [date], the court issued a default judgment in the claim concerning [subject]. I did not attend because [specific reason]. I actually obtained the judgment on [date] by [method].

Postal item [identifier] was not served on me. The tracking report shows no attempted delivery / the envelope used [incorrect address] instead of [correct address]. Objective obstacles are proved by [certificate or official response]. I did not know of the judgment before [date] and filed without delay on [date].

Under Article 112 of the Civil Procedure Code, a time limit missed for a justified reason may be restored. Supreme Court determination No. 24-KG26-8-K4 of 10 November 2026 distinguishes avoidance by an addressee from non-delivery for reasons outside their control.

The judgment should also be set aside because my absence was caused by [reason], and the following arguments and evidence may affect the decision: [specific defence].

I REQUEST:
1. Restore the time limit for applying to set aside the default judgment dated [date] in case [number].
2. Set aside the default judgment and resume examination on the merits.
3. Admit the attached evidence.

Attachments: judgment copy; envelope; tracking report; certificates; proof of date of receipt; evidence on the merits; copies for participants; other documents required by the current Civil Procedure Code.
[Date] [Signature]

This model must be adapted to the correct court, current time limits, grounds for setting aside, service on other participants and any enforcement already under way.

Practical conclusion

A persuasive application proves the delivery failure and a prompt response after discovering the judgment; it does not stop at “I did not receive mail.” Case No. 24-KG26-8-K4 directs attention to who made the address error, whether delivery was attempted, and whether non-receipt was attributable to the defendant. It does not remove the need for a defence to the underlying claim.
