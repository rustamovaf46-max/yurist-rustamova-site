Restoring the deadline to accept an inheritance in Russia

Русский · English · 中文 · العربية

Fatima Rustamova · Published 13 October 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

If more than six months have passed since the inheritance opened, first check whether you actually missed the deadline. An heir may have accepted the inheritance by their conduct within the original period. Other options are to obtain the consent of the heirs who have already accepted or to apply to the court.

Where to start if you may have missed the deadline

Under Article 1154 of the Russian Civil Code, the general period for accepting an inheritance is six months from the date it opens. Special periods apply on certain grounds. Establish the relevant date and calculate the period using the documents. The date of your first visit to a notary does not determine when the period starts.

For an initial assessment, identify the date of death, the basis on which you inherit, any other heirs and what you did during the first months after the inheritance opened. Establish when you learned that it had opened and what prevented you from taking the necessary steps.

Three ways to establish your inheritance rights

1. Check for acceptance by conduct

Article 1153 of the Russian Civil Code allows an inheritance to be accepted through conduct. Relevant actions may include taking possession of estate property, taking steps to preserve it or paying for its upkeep at your own expense within the acceptance period. An heir who did not apply to a notary may still have accepted in time.

Identify the estate property to which the actions related, their dates and the documents that support them. The evidence and the existence of any dispute determine whether the inheritance can be documented by a notary or an appropriate court claim is required.

2. Obtain written consent from the other heirs

Article 1155(2) permits acceptance outside court with the written consent of all the other heirs who have already accepted the inheritance. The signatures must meet the legal formalities. Verbal consent or consent from only some of those heirs is insufficient.

3. Bring a claim in court

Article 1155(1) requires the court to consider the reasons for missing the original deadline and whether the heir filed after those reasons ceased within the period allowed by law. The claim should ask the court to restore the deadline and recognise the claimant as having accepted the inheritance.

This is a contentious civil claim. Paragraph 40 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 identifies the heirs who acquired the inheritance as the defendants, including where certificates have yet to be issued. If the estate is escheat property, the relevant public entity must be joined. A notary's involvement in the inheritance file alone does not make the notary the proper sole defendant.

Which reasons can the court accept?

Paragraph 40 of the Plenum of the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation's Resolution No. 9 of 29 May 2012 addresses cases where the heir neither knew nor should have known that the inheritance had opened, and cases involving other valid reasons personal to the heir. Serious illness or a condition making the person unable to act must have prevented acceptance throughout the applicable acceptance period. Explain which steps the condition prevented and for how long, with evidence.

A brief illness, unfamiliarity with inheritance rules or a lack of information about particular estate assets is generally insufficient on its own. Prepare a dated account and supporting documents.

A separate six-month deadline applies. File in court within six months after the reasons for the delay cease to apply. Paragraph 40 of Resolution No. 9 states that this special deadline cannot be restored. Keep documents showing the date it started.

What the claimant needs to prove

Article 1155 and paragraph 40 require proof of two distinct points: why the claimant validly missed the original acceptance deadline, and that the claim was filed no later than six months after those reasons ceased. Medical evidence of a sustained obstacle still leaves the date on which it ended to be proved.

For a prolonged illness, the court assesses whether the condition actually prevented the heir from taking legally effective steps throughout the acceptance period. The court can consider whether an application could have reached the notary through a representative or whether the heir could have sent a duly certified application by post. The evidence should address the heir's ability to express the intention to accept.

Prepare two timelines. The first covers the entire original acceptance period. The second covers the time from the end of the obstacle to filing in court. Explain any gap between the dates and substantiate the explanation.

Two situations can call for different remedies

Suppose the deceased died on 10 January. An heir who neither knew nor should have known that the inheritance had opened receives reliable information about the death on 20 August. If those facts are proved, the special six-month period under Article 1155 ends on 20 February of the following year, subject to the general rules for calculating time limits. Preserve proof of when the information was received. The date of a notary's refusal does not necessarily mark the end of the obstacle.

Suppose another heir continued to possess the deceased's property, secured it and paid for its upkeep during the original acceptance period. First assess acceptance by conduct under Article 1153(2), as restoring the deadline may be the wrong remedy. A receipt alone does not establish acceptance. The court assesses the acts, their dates and whether the heir treated the property as their own.

If all the heirs who accepted the inheritance agree to the late heir's acceptance, assess the procedure and written consent requirements under Article 1155(2). If consent is unavailable, the appropriate court remedy depends on the facts the heir can prove.

What the Supreme Court requires the judgment to resolve

Under paragraphs 40–41 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9, the heirs who acquired the inheritance are the proper defendants. When restoring the deadline, the court must recognise the claimant as having accepted, determine all heirs' shares, protect the new heir's share where necessary and address whether previously issued certificates are invalid. A claim that only requests restoration may leave important issues unresolved.

Defendants often challenge the dates. They may argue that the claimant could have learned of the death, had means of communication, could have sent an application to a notary or obtained information earlier than claimed. Use documents, correspondence, health records and a consistent timeline to answer these points. A lack of information about a particular flat does not establish that the heir was unaware that the inheritance had opened.

Sample claim to restore the deadline to accept an inheritance

This outline is for an heir who did not accept within the period by applying to a notary or by conduct and has grounds under Article 1155(1). Evidence of timely acceptance by conduct calls for a different remedy. See establishing acceptance by conduct or bringing a claim where inheritance rights are disputed (Russian).

I file this claim on [date] within six months after the reason ceased, as shown by [supporting evidence].

On the basis of Articles 1154 and 1155 of the Russian Civil Code, Articles 131–132 of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure and paragraphs 40–41 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 of 29 May 2012, I ask the court to:

  1. Restore the deadline for me to accept the inheritance of [full name, date of death].
  2. Recognise me as having accepted the inheritance opened by the death of [full name].
  3. Determine the heirs' shares in the estate [proposed allocation with calculations and legal grounds].
  4. Declare previously issued inheritance certificates invalid [certificate details, in full or in relevant part], if issued and affecting the rights claimed.
  5. [Where necessary and legally supported, specify a measure to protect the share that accounts for what forms part of the estate and what has happened to it.]

Attachments

  • Proof of death and entitlement to inherit, inheritance file information and property documents.
  • Evidence of the reason for missing the deadline, how long it lasted and the date the obstacle ended.
  • Share calculations and proof of payment of the court fee or entitlement to an exemption.
  • Proof that copies of the claim and any attachments the other parties lack were sent to them.
  • Power of attorney, if applicable, and other supporting evidence.

[Date] · [Signature of claimant or authorised representative]

Jurisdiction and limits of the sample. A claim is usually brought in the district or city court where the defendant lives. If relief concerning rights to immovable property is sought, check the exclusive territorial jurisdiction rules under Article 30 of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure and Plenum Resolution No. 9. The court fee depends on the claims and the current version of Article 333.19 of the Russian Tax Code. If the estate is deemed escheat property, the proper defendants change. If the property has already been transferred, assess the consequences under Article 1155, including whether compensation may be available in place of return of the property.

For an assessment of the claim and the relief to request, see representation in inheritance disputes. Dates and the available documents can be discussed first by telephone or in MAX.

Documents to prepare

  1. Entitlement to inherit. Proof of the death, kinship or the will and information about the estate.
  2. Notarial file. Available information about the heirs' applications, certificates issued, your contacts with the notary and any responses.
  3. Reasons for delay. Medical or other records showing the obstacle and how long it lasted.
  4. Key dates. Proof of when you learned that the inheritance had opened, when the obstacle ended and when you first sought relief.
  5. Actions involving the property. Receipts, contracts, correspondence and other material showing possession, upkeep or preservation.

Prepare a clear timeline and the supporting records that relate to your dispute. The documents required depend on your grounds and the remedy you seek.

What happens if the court grants the claim?

Restoration affects the rights of the other heirs. Under Article 1155 and paragraph 41 of Plenum Resolution No. 9, the court determines inheritance shares and addresses certificates already issued. Identify which heirs have completed the inheritance formalities and obtain details of the documents before filing.

Frequently asked questions

Have I permanently lost the inheritance after six months?

Check whether you accepted by conduct within the original period, whether all the other heirs who accepted will give consent, or whether the conditions for a court to restore the deadline are met.

Is saying that I did not know about the inheritance enough?

You need evidence of the circumstances and when you obtained the information. The court distinguishes between being unaware that the inheritance opened and being unaware of a particular asset.

Do I need to go to court if the other heirs agree?

Article 1155 allows acceptance outside court if all the other heirs who have accepted the inheritance give written consent in the required form.

How do I prepare for an initial consultation?

Record when the inheritance opened, when you learned about it and when the obstacle ended. Gather documents on your entitlement, available materials from the notary and proof of your actions involving the estate.
