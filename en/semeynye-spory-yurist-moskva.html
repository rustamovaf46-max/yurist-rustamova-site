Which family disputes can be brought to court?

Where a child will live and parental rights

When parents live separately, the child's best interests are central. The court considers the actual upbringing environment, daily routine, attachments, each parent's participation and other relevant circumstances. Higher income or a larger home does not automatically decide the dispute. The applicable framework includes Article 65 of the Family Code of the Russian Federation and Resolution No. 10 of the Plenum of the Supreme Court of 27 May 1998. See the guide to child residence disputes in Russian courts.

Parent–child contact schedules and enforcement

The child's age, health, education and rest schedule, distance between parental homes, existing relationships and whether a proposed schedule can realistically be followed all matter. Requested arrangements should specify days, hours, holidays, handovers and communication methods. Article 66 of the Russian Family Code is relevant. See the Supreme Court review of child-upbringing disputes and guide to parent–child contact arrangements in Russian courts.

Child maintenance: income shares, fixed amounts and combined awards

Irregular or changing earnings, lack of employment or an income-share award that fails to protect the child's interests may justify examining a fixed monetary award or a combined method where statutory conditions are met. Under Article 83 of the Family Code, evidence of the child's expenses and both parties' financial circumstances is important. An absence of officially declared earnings does not automatically entitle a claimant to the amount requested. Read how to substantiate a fixed maintenance amount.

Division of matrimonial property, loans and debts

Identify when assets were acquired, the source of purchase funds, whether borrowing served family needs and what payments were made after the relationship ended. A loan taken by one spouse is not automatically a joint debt. The division of marital debts and limitation periods for property division require separate fact-specific analysis.

Documents to prepare before court

  1. Chronology: dates of marriage, separation, children's births, acquisition of assets and key disputed events.
  2. Child-related records: relevant certificates, education and health information where necessary, evidence of parental involvement, correspondence and housing circumstances.
  3. Financial records: documented earnings, child-related expenses, agreements, receipts, property and banking records, and liabilities. Avoid unnecessary disclosure of personal data.
  4. Earlier proceedings: parental agreements, enforcement documents, judgments, bailiff records and guardianship-authority responses where relevant.

How court representation is organised

After reviewing the case materials, the representative identifies the relief sought and its legal basis, jurisdiction, participants, evidence and any interim protection that may be needed. Child-related cases may require participation by guardianship authorities and, where justified, specialist assessments or examination of living conditions. Financial disputes may require requests for income, property or evidence of how borrowed funds were used.

The agreed mandate may include drafting claims and objections, gathering and assessing evidence, attending hearings, handling expert evidence and analysing the judgment. Appeal, enforcement proceedings, expert fees and other stages are agreed separately unless expressly included in the engagement.

Risks and prospects

Family judgments depend on the specific facts. A favourable Supreme Court decision in another matter does not guarantee the same outcome here. The child's interests guide decisions about residence and contact. Maintenance amounts require financial evidence, and marital-debt claims require evidence of the loan's purpose and how the funds were used. I review the case file and explain the weaknesses and litigation risks I identify.

At what stage is your case?

When contacting the representative, identify the court, the next hearing date or the appeal deadline.

  • Before filing: review the evidence, claims, jurisdiction and procedural risks.
  • Proceedings pending: assess objections, motions, expert evidence, hearing preparation and representation.
  • Judgment issued: examine deadlines and grounds for appeal or cassation.
  • Judgment final: consider enforcement, recovery of legal costs and further remedies.

Child maintenance and establishing paternity

In a paternity dispute, identify the evidence already filed and check whether the court has ordered genetic testing. A private DNA test does not automatically substitute for evidence assessed under Russian civil procedure. In a maintenance dispute, assess income, the child's needs and the legal grounds for an income share, a fixed sum or a combination. In your first enquiry, include the court, the stage of the case, the nearest deadline and a list of available records.
