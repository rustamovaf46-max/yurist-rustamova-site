Russian Supreme Court: waterfront land and good-faith buyers

Complete English-language analytical translation of the Russian court-practice review. This is an editorial analysis of a reported judgment, not a claim that the site owner acted in the case. The procedural example requires adaptation to the individual dispute and cannot replace legal advice.

A plot may have been bought in good faith and registered in the Unified State Register of Real Estate (EGRN), yet still include part of a public shoreline strip that cannot lawfully be privatised. The Supreme Court of the Russian Federation distinguishes that situation from a plot merely situated in a water-protection zone. The classification must be proved using lawful boundaries and reliable technical evidence.

Principal conclusion: registration and an honest purchase do not remove an express statutory bar to privatisation of public shoreline land. Conversely, the claimant must first prove an actual overlap with the shoreline strip: the mere existence of another environmental zone is insufficient.

A registered purchase does not cure every land restriction

A buyer may acquire an individually registered plot and later face a claim that part of it overlaps the public shoreline strip of a water body. Registration in EGRN and good faith are important facts but do not by themselves legalise land that Russian law prohibits from privatisation.

The Supreme Court case

Item 1 of the Supreme Court Review of Judicial Practice No. 1 (2026) addresses two plots classified as settlement land and designated for individual housing construction, acquired by the owner in 2013. Following an investigation in 2022, the environmental prosecutor alleged that the boundaries overlapped the public shoreline strip of the Klyazma Reservoir and other specially regulated zones. Geodetic surveying produced a diagram with coordinates and the area of the alleged overlap.

The trial court granted the prosecutor’s claim. The appellate and cassation courts disagreed, relying in particular on the buyer’s good faith, registration of ownership and limitation arguments. By determination No. 4-KGPR25-48-K1, the Supreme Court set aside those rulings and reinstated the trial court decision. The outcome was not based on the proposition that every water-protection zone excludes private property: the decisive factual finding was that part of these privately formed plots was within land reserved as a publicly accessible shoreline strip.

Shoreline strip is not a water-protection zone

These separate legal regimes should not be conflated. Under Article 6(6) of the Russian Water Code, a public shoreline strip is ordinarily twenty metres wide for the relevant publicly accessible water bodies, subject to legal exceptions and the characteristics of the particular water body. Article 27(8) of the Russian Land Code prohibits privatisation of land plots within the established public shoreline strip.

A water-protection zone is different: it regulates economic and other activities but its existence alone does not establish that every privately owned plot inside it is unlawfully privatised. A coastal protective strip within a water-protection zone may be subject to stricter restrictions. Sanitary protection zones for drinking-water sources constitute yet another legal regime. For each restriction, it is necessary to establish its actual boundaries, the date and legal basis of their establishment, the rules applicable to the specific site, and how they intersect with the disputed cadastral coordinates. Substituting one regime for another can undermine both a claim and an expert assessment.

Good faith and the land register

The Supreme Court's reasoning does not mean every plot near a reservoir belongs to the state. The claimant must prove that the specific land actually falls within a legally protected public shoreline strip. Good-faith purchase and registration cannot override a direct statutory privatisation prohibition once that factual and legal basis is established.

Limitation periods require factual analysis

In the case reviewed, the prosecutor identified the violation in August 2022 and filed suit in December 2022. The Supreme Court noted that there was no evidence demonstrating that the competent state authority had known, or should have known, of the violation earlier. On those particular facts, the limitation objection did not succeed.

This does not mean that limitation periods are categorically inapplicable to all claims involving shoreline land. The court must identify the precise remedy, the proper rights-holder, the applicable rules for computing time and the evidence of when the relevant authority learned or should have learned of the infringement. Simply counting three years from the purchaser’s acquisition is not a complete limitation analysis.

Evidence and technical examination

Important evidence includes a current EGRN extract with coordinates; historical records on the formation of each plot; boundary plans, cadastral and land-surveying files; official information about the shoreline and the water body’s legal status; State Water Register materials; geodetic overlay diagrams; and decisions or records establishing water-protection and sanitary protection zones. It is necessary to compare both the legal bases for restrictions and the source maps, dates, coordinate systems and measurement tolerances.

If parties dispute the intersection diagram, identify the exact technical matters requiring examination: the location of the shoreline on a legally relevant date, whether the plot overlaps the shoreline strip, the coordinates and area of any intersection, and whether competing diagrams use reliable input data. Land-surveying and hydrological expertise may help resolve those technical points. Expert witnesses do not determine whether the land legally belongs to the state; that conclusion is reserved to the court.

In the case analysed, the owner did not rebut the overlap diagram and declined a proposed combined land-surveying and hydrological examination. A party who disputes the scheme should therefore provide verifiable counter-data rather than relying only on general criticism, and should submit a reasoned expert-examination motion at the appropriate procedural stage.

Procedural strategy

Identify the exact claim, the legal regime asserted and the area of alleged overlap. Compare official maps and coordinate systems, test the claimant's methodology and consider whether independent evidence or expert examination is necessary. A bare assertion that the buyer acted in good faith may leave the decisive factual issue unanswered.

Sample motion for a combined land-surveying and hydrological expert examination

To: [name of court]
Case No.: [number]
Claimant: [prosecutor / public authority / name]
Respondent: [name and address]

MOTION FOR APPOINTMENT OF A COMBINED LAND-SURVEYING AND HYDROLOGICAL EXPERT EXAMINATION

The dispute concerns the recovery of [part of] cadastral plot [number], based on an alleged overlap with the public shoreline strip of [water body]. The claimant relies on a diagram dated [date] prepared by [organisation]. I consider the technical evidence incomplete because [specific coordinate discrepancy, map source problem, survey tolerance or missing underlying data].

Under Article 79 of the Civil Procedure Code of the Russian Federation, I request that the court appoint a combined land-surveying and hydrological expert examination, to be entrusted to [competent expert organisation / named qualified expert], subject to verification of the expert’s competence and availability.

Questions proposed for the experts:

  1. On the basis of the legal, technical and source documents supplied by the court, what were the coordinates of the shoreline of [water body] on the legally relevant date [date], insofar as they can be determined?
  2. Do the boundaries of plot [number] intersect the applicable public shoreline strip? If so, what are the intersection coordinates and area?
  3. Which source materials and measurement methods support the calculation, and are there material discrepancies between the parties’ diagrams?
  4. Are there technically feasible alternatives for correcting any proven overlap by adjusting boundaries, and what would their technical consequences be?

I request that the court add this motion to the case file, hear the parties on the scope of questions and proposed experts, identify the materials to be examined, and determine the procedure for advance payment in accordance with procedural law and the court’s ruling. If legally justified, I request allocation of advance expenses in light of [case-specific reasons].

Attachments: [diagrams, cadastral plans, EGRN extracts, specialist opinions, correspondence and details of the proposed expert organisation].
[Date] [Signature]

This is a procedural example, not a filed document. The evidence, proposed questions, court, parties and payment request must be adapted to the case.

Special warning for landowners

Before buying land by a water body, investigate more than an EGRN extract: the water-law regime, official boundaries of public land and the actual shoreline configuration may prove decisive. In litigation the question may not be whether building is allowed, but whether the land can lawfully be privately owned at all. These questions require different remedies and evidence. Depending on the circumstances, separate potential claims against a seller, cadastral engineer or public authority may merit assessment, but neither their grounds nor their limitation periods arise automatically from losing a property dispute.
