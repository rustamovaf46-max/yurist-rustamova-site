Disputes with the Social Fund of Russia

Fatima Rustamova provides representation in disputes concerning Russian pension rights and decisions of the Social Fund of Russia (SFR). The correct legal route depends on the pension type, the refusal, the applicable rules, employment records and procedural deadlines.

Refusal to award a pension

A refusal may concern eligibility, insufficient qualifying service, pension coefficients or missing documentation. The written decision should be obtained and analysed against the applicable legislation and the person's employment and insurance records. A refusal should not be challenged without identifying its actual grounds.

Confirming employment and special service

Evidence may include employment books, archival certificates, employer records, individual account data, orders and other documents. Early retirement cases can require proof of particular work conditions, job titles and periods under rules applicable to those periods. Not every period of work counts identically for every pension.

Recalculation and payment arrears

Disputes may concern the amount awarded, omitted periods, indexation or the effective date of payment. The claim must distinguish correction of an administrative calculation from establishment of a disputed right. Arrears and interest are not automatic; the applicable statutory basis must be assessed.

Pre-court review and jurisdiction

The SFR decision, any administrative complaint and correspondence should be preserved. Depending on the dispute, procedural law determines the competent court, required steps and time limits. A request for clarification or an administrative appeal should not be assumed to suspend every judicial deadline.

Evidence and expert issues

Collect the written SFR refusal, individual personal account information, employment records, archival responses, pension calculations and any prior judgments. Where documents conflict, identify the precise period or legal fact in dispute before drafting the claim.

Court representation and fees

Services can include legal assessment, requests for evidence, statements of claim, objections, motions, hearings and appeal analysis. Court representation starts at RUB 50,000 per instance. State duties and other external costs, if applicable, are discussed separately. No result is guaranteed.

Employment periods missing from the pension record

Work history should be reconstructed chronologically, identifying each disputed employer, position, employment date and the legal requirements that applied at that time. Compare employment books, appointment and dismissal orders, archival certificates, payslips, insurance-contribution information and the insured person's individual account (ILS). A missing entry in the ILS does not automatically prove that the underlying work never occurred. The applicable evidence rules may differ between periods before and after registration in the individual-account system, and whether witness testimony may be used must be assessed under the relevant law.

First ask the employer or archive for the missing record and consider whether the ILS can be corrected. When asking a court to include a period in insured service, identify the exact start and end dates, the type of qualifying service sought, the reason for the SFR exclusion and the documents that contradict that reason.

Early retirement and preferential service

An early pension requires proof of the particular statutory category, job title, actual duties, work conditions and required period under the relevant rules and occupational lists. A position with a similar name may not suffice if the legally required character or conditions of the work are not proved. Where periods span different legislative regimes, examine the law and counting rules applying to each period and the possible transitional rules. Employer clarifications, personnel files, working-time records and archived work-condition evidence may be important.

Do not ask merely to “recognise a preferential pension” without specifying the periods, the relevant list, the legal clause and the evidentiary reason each period should be included.

Challenging an SFR decision in court

Obtain the territorial SFR's dated written decision, complete payment or registration case materials, individual-account information and calculations. Compare the refusal against the primary records, legislation in force at each material date and the right that should have arisen. The potential remedy may be inclusion of specified periods, recognition of pension entitlement, correction of the amount or an obligation to perform a particular act. Distinguish a pension-rights dispute dealt with through civil proceedings from other public-law challenges: not every SFR refusal must be brought as an administrative claim under the Russian Code of Administrative Judicial Procedure. The Supreme Court's review of pension disputes approved on 26 June 2024 describes the range of civil claims.

The applicant should also establish from what date the alleged right arose. The fact that a higher pension is ultimately justified does not automatically mean that all possible arrears are payable from any date selected by the claimant. Keep the pension application, delivery confirmations, subsequent correspondence and all responses; assess procedural time limits and available correction routes in the individual case.

Document checklist and work plan for representation

  1. Identify the written refusal, the exact pension type and every disputed work period, coefficient or payment component.
  2. Request and cross-check the ILS, employment book, employer orders, archival certificates, salary and contribution records, specialist-service certificates and earlier SFR correspondence.
  3. Assess which missing facts can be corrected without litigation and what information must be requested from an employer, archive or SFR; consider narrowly framed motions for evidence or expertise when necessary.
  4. Determine the correct court and procedural form, the persons who must participate, the precise requested remedy, applicable deadlines and court fees or exemptions.
  5. Prepare the claim, objections and motions; represent the client in hearings; analyse the judgment, appeal options and any later litigation costs according to the agreed scope.
