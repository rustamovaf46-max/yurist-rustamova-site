Proving acceptance of an inheritance by conduct in Russia

Русский · English · 中文 · العربية

Fatima Rustamova · Published 27 October 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

An heir can accept an inheritance by applying to a notary or by conduct, such as taking possession of estate property, paying for its upkeep, protecting it from claims and treating it as their own. Timely acts of acceptance can preserve inheritance rights even without an application. The question later is what the heir did and when.

Proof of acceptance by conduct concerns what the heir did within the original acceptance period. Restoring a missed deadline requires valid grounds for the delay and a timely court claim after the obstacle ended. Choosing the wrong remedy can lead to dismissal.

When disputes arise

A dispute often emerges after six months have passed since the death. The heir may have continued to live in a flat, repaired a house, worked a plot or paid required charges without opening an inheritance file or applying in time. A notary may find the documents insufficient, other heirs may dispute the acts, a municipality may claim the estate as escheat property, or Rosreestr may need adequate title documents before registering ownership.

Under Article 1152 of the Russian Civil Code, an accepted inheritance belongs to the heir from the date the inheritance opens, regardless of when a certificate is issued or the right is registered. Dealing with a flat, house or land usually requires documentary confirmation of title.

Acts that can establish acceptance

Article 1153(2) gives a non-exhaustive list. Paragraph 36 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 focuses on possession, use, management and preservation that show the heir treated estate property as their own.

  • Possession and use. Living in the flat or house, moving in, using a vehicle or working a plot.
  • Preservation. Repairing damage after an incident, replacing locks, securing the house or resisting unlawful claims by third parties.
  • Upkeep at the heir's expense. Paying utilities, taxes, insurance, repairs or necessary work.
  • Management and protection. Collecting money due to the deceased, recovering a debt or applying to protect estate assets.
  • Discharge of obligations. Using personal funds to pay documented debts of the deceased.

Acceptance of part of the property due on a particular basis entails acceptance of all the inheritance due on that basis. Kinship, an existing share in jointly owned property or payment for the funeral does not by itself show acceptance of the estate.

Why the first six months matter

Under Article 1154, the general acceptance period is six months from the opening of the inheritance. Identify at least one legally relevant act within that period. Payments or repairs begun much later cannot substitute for proof of timely conduct.

An heir who accepted by conduct in time can seek a certificate after the six months have passed. As time passes, documents, transaction records and witnesses may become harder to locate. If there were no timely acts of acceptance, Article 1155 provides a separate route with different conditions and deadlines.

Evidence to gather

Connect the heir, the property and the date of the act. A receipt without an address or the payer's name is usually weaker than several records that support one another.

  • Residence-registration extracts and proof of living with the deceased.
  • Utility, tax and insurance receipts and bank statements.
  • Repair contracts, completion records and receipts that identify the property.
  • Records of security measures, changed locks, emergency work or upkeep of land.
  • Correspondence with the property manager, utility supplier, Rosreestr or a court.
  • Photographs, messages and electronic records with verifiable dates.
  • Witness testimony to supplement written and digital records.

Prepare a timeline showing the date of death and the acceptance period. List each act, any amount paid and the corresponding proof. This helps identify what is missing before going to a notary or court.

Does registration at the flat suffice?

Residence registration does not automatically confer inheritance rights. In its decision No. 25-P of 27 May 2024, the Constitutional Court held that lawful registration in the disputed dwelling when the inheritance opened, continuing when the dispute arose, should be taken into account with the other facts.

Registration can strengthen evidence of actual residence, payment of expenses, preservation of the property and absence of renunciation. A registration address alone may be insufficient where the heir did not use the dwelling.

When to go to a notary or to court

Normally start with the notary at the place where the inheritance opened and present proof of acceptance by conduct. If the notary considers the evidence sufficient and the heirs do not dispute the right, the inheritance can be documented without a court proceeding.

If undisputed documentation is lacking, the notary explains the court route. In special proceedings, the court establishes a legal fact where there is no dispute over the right. A civil claim is needed where another heir or public owner disputes the right, or where the relief includes recognition of ownership, determination of shares or a challenge to certificates already issued.

If a dispute about rights emerges during special proceedings, the court leaves the application without consideration and explains that a claim may be brought. First check the inheritance file, certificates issued, any claim by the municipality and third-party rights already registered.

Relief to consider

Some cases only require a finding of acceptance. Others also require recognition of ownership of a flat or share, a determination of the estate's composition, invalidation of an earlier certificate or cancellation of a competing entry in the Unified State Register of Real Estate.

Identify the right affected, the proper defendant and the remedy that will allow the title to be documented. Check exclusive territorial jurisdiction for claims concerning rights to immovable property.

What the courts emphasise

Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 directs courts to assess what the heir actually did. Timely conduct must show an intention to accept the inheritance. Constitutional Court decision No. 25-P explains that residence registration has to be weighed alongside the other facts, particularly in a long-running dispute about a dwelling claimed as escheat.

The Supreme Court's official report of 27 February 2026 states that where several heirs inherit a flat without allocation of specific parts, they acquire shared ownership from the opening of the inheritance. If a judgment has already established acceptance in equal shares, asking for a separate document confirming shared ownership should not become an unjustified barrier to registration.

Mistakes that weaken a case

  • Asking to restore the deadline when the heir accepted by conduct in time.
  • Submitting payment records that do not identify the address, date or connection with the heir.
  • Relying solely on residence registration, kinship or arranging the funeral.
  • Failing to check for other heirs and certificates already issued.
  • Using special proceedings where ownership is clearly disputed.
  • Asking for a finding of fact while omitting further relief needed to register ownership.
  • Leaving out interested parties or existing rights holders.

How to prove acceptance when the notary sees only late payments

The certificate does not have to be issued in the first six months. The court needs evidence of conduct within that period. Paying for a flat two years after the death shows later upkeep. Living with the deceased in the flat at the date of death may be relevant even without registration there.

What Supreme Court guidance establishes

Paragraphs 34–37 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 address three points. Rights arise from the opening of an accepted inheritance. Acceptance by conduct requires timely acts that show the heir treated the property as their own. Co-ownership with the deceased and receipt of a burial allowance do not by themselves show acceptance.

Show the property, the act, its date, the source of funds and the heir's intention. A contract to repair a leak in the inherited flat, signed by the heir during the six months, can be more useful than a much later payment that does not identify the payer.

Objections to check first

The other party may say the heir occupied the flat as a tenant or family member, paid for a different owner, acted only after the deadline, or used property that was not due to the heir on the relevant basis. Paragraph 35 of Resolution No. 9 does not automatically extend acceptance on one ground of succession to property due on another ground. Paragraph 37 allows evidence that the acts served another purpose and did not express an intention to accept.

Ask the property manager to identify the payer and any requests made, obtain bank statements for the first six months, establish the date the keys were handed over and review the inheritance file. Ask witnesses about specific events.

Sample petition to establish acceptance of an inheritance

Use this sample in special proceedings where there is no dispute over rights. If an heir disputes entitlement or a claim challenges another person's registered title, identify the proper defendants and prepare a civil claim. Article 266 of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure generally requires this petition to be filed with the district court at the applicant's residence. The exception for establishing possession and use of immovable property concerns a different legal fact. Determine jurisdiction for a property claim separately, including Article 30.

To [name of district court at the applicant's place of residence]

Applicant: [full name, date and place of birth, address, identifier if available and known, phone and email]

Interested parties: [other heirs, notary handling the file, anyone else whose rights may be affected, and their addresses]

Petition to establish the fact of acceptance of an inheritance

On [date], [full name of the deceased] died, as confirmed by [death certificate details]. I inherit [under the law, with kinship and supporting documents / under the will, with details]. The inheritance opened at [address]. The estate includes [description, title information and cadastral number if available].

Within the time allowed by Article 1154 of the Russian Civil Code, I took the following acts showing acceptance by conduct: [first act, exact date, property and document], [second act, exact date and document]. I managed and maintained the estate property as my own. The evidence includes [list of documents and witness details].

I applied to [notary's name, district, file number and date] for a certificate of entitlement to inherit. [State the response and why the documents do not suffice for recognition outside court.] The finding is necessary for [obtaining the certificate / other specific legal purpose]. I cannot obtain an appropriate document establishing the fact outside court because [specific reasons]. There is no dispute over rights between the interested parties [include this statement only if true].

Under Articles 1152–1154 of the Russian Civil Code, Articles 262–268 of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure and paragraph 36 of Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 9 of 29 May 2012, I ask the court to:

  1. Establish that I, [full name], accepted the inheritance opened by the death of [full name of the deceased] on [date] [under the law / under the will].
  2. If needed, obtain [specific documents, relevant period and reasons they cannot be obtained independently] from [notary / property manager / bank], on a separate reasoned application.
  3. Summon and hear [witnesses' names, addresses and the facts they know], on the relevant application.

Attachments. Proof of the court fee, exemption or request for relief from the fee, death certificate, proof of kinship or the will, estate records, proof of acts within the period, the notary's response and details of the inheritance file, proof of sending copies to interested parties where applicable, the representative's authority where applicable, and other relevant records.

[Date] · [Signature] · [Full name]

Before filing, check the current court fee under the Russian Tax Code, the proceeding required, the interested parties and the evidence. A petition of this kind does not resolve ownership or remove competing rights from the Unified State Register of Real Estate.

If a dispute emerges

A court should not resolve competing claims to a flat or escheat property in proceedings to establish a legal fact. Under Article 263(3) of the Russian Code of Civil Procedure, the application is left without consideration and the parties are informed of the right to bring a claim. Name the defendants, explain the right infringed and remedy sought, prove service of copies and meet Articles 131–132. Where the subject is a right to immovable property, check exclusive jurisdiction at its location.

Frequently asked questions

Is a single utility payment enough?

The law sets no minimum number of acts. The court assesses the evidence together. One receipt may prove acceptance if it identifies who paid, when and for what property. Add evidence of possession and upkeep where available.

Can the inheritance be documented years later?

If timely acceptance by conduct is proved, the passage of time does not in itself cancel the right. The difficulty is preserving evidence and addressing the potential rights of other people.

Must an heir who lived in the flat restore the deadline?

If residence and other acts establish timely acceptance, the case concerns proof of that fact. The deadline-restoration route applies in different circumstances.

Are witnesses enough?

Witness testimony is admissible. In a property dispute, combine it with written, bank and digital records where possible. The court examines relevance, reliability and the link to the original period.

Who will be the defendant?

Depending on the dispute, the defendants may be heirs who received certificates, a public entity claiming escheat property or another person contesting the right. If there is no dispute, special proceedings can involve interested parties.
