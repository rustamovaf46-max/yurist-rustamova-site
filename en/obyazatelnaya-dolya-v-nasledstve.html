The compulsory share of a Russian estate: eligibility and calculation

Русский · English · 中文 · العربية

A will may leave an apartment, bank deposit or other property to someone else. Some relatives remain entitled to a compulsory share. To calculate it, identify who would inherit under the law, what makes up the entire estate and what the eligible heir already receives from the estate on other grounds.

Minimum compulsory share = one half of that heir’s statutory share. The calculation starts with property that the will does not dispose of. Property left under the will is used to cover any shortfall.

Who is entitled to a compulsory share?

Article 1149(1) of the Russian Civil Code identifies four categories:

  • the deceased’s minor children;
  • the deceased’s children who are classed as unable to work for inheritance purposes;
  • the deceased’s spouse and parents who are classed as unable to work for inheritance purposes;
  • dependants who are unable to work and qualify to inherit under Article 1148 of the Russian Civil Code.

Eligibility is assessed on the date the inheritance opens. For these inheritance rules, the statutory age thresholds remain 55 for women and 60 for men. Disability is evidenced by the officially established disability category. For dependants, check the duration and nature of the support and, where required by law, whether they lived with the deceased.

Being related or having a close personal relationship is insufficient on its own. An adult child who is able to work, a former spouse or an unmarried partner does not qualify on that basis alone. An unmarried partner may claim as a dependant who is unable to work only if the conditions of Article 1148 are proved.

How to calculate the compulsory share

  1. Identify all property and property rights that belonged to the deceased on the date of death.
  2. For assets acquired during marriage, establish the marital share first. Include the deceased’s share of jointly owned property in the estate.
  3. Identify all heirs who would be called to inherit without a will, including heirs by right of representation where applicable.
  4. Calculate the eligible heir’s statutory share.
  5. Take half of that statutory share. Credit everything the heir receives from the estate on other grounds, including the value of a testamentary legacy (завещательный отказ).

Example. Without a will, a spouse and two children would inherit in the first priority class. Each would receive one third of the estate. If one child is a minor, the child’s compulsory share is at least one sixth of the whole estate, or half of one third. The value of the assets and the rights the child already receives can affect how the share is delivered. The calculation principle stays the same.

Which property is used to provide the share?

Use the part of the estate that is not disposed of by the will first, even if that reduces what other statutory heirs receive. Property left under the will is affected when the unallocated estate is insufficient.

The calculation covers the value of the entire estate, including real estate, money, vehicles, company interests, exclusive rights and other assets. A dispute about the share can therefore turn on the composition and value of the estate. Real estate, a business or valuable belongings may require valuation as of the date the inheritance opened.

Does the heir have to accept the inheritance?

The share must be claimed. The heir must accept the inheritance within the prescribed period and tell the notary the basis of the claim, with documents showing the relevant family relationship, age, disability or dependency. The general period for acceptance is six months from the opening of the inheritance.

When the period is missed, assess actual acceptance and extension of the period separately. See restoring the time limit to accept an inheritance for the conditions and proof required.

When can a court reduce or deny the compulsory share?

A court can apply the exception in Article 1149(4) when all the following conditions are met:

  • granting the share would prevent the property from passing to the heir named in the will, and the compulsory heir did not use it during the deceased’s lifetime;
  • the heir named in the will used the property as a home or as their main source of livelihood;
  • the parties’ financial circumstances and other facts justify reducing or denying the share.

A party asking for a reduction or denial must file the relevant claim and prove their use of the property, the compulsory heir’s lack of use, why the property cannot pass as intended, and the parties’ financial circumstances. Living in the only home at issue does not by itself settle the question.

Claiming a compulsory share and contesting the will

The will remains valid when the compulsory share is granted. Its terms are implemented subject to the protected heir’s entitlement. Contesting the will involves separate grounds of invalidity, such as defective form, an inability to understand the significance of one’s actions, fraud or coercion. See contesting a will in court and the evidence required (Russian).

Documents and evidence to prepare

  • death certificate and documents showing kinship or marriage;
  • the minor child’s birth certificate;
  • evidence of disability status or age;
  • for a dependant, information on income, transfers, expenses, shared household arrangements and cohabitation;
  • the will or information about it from the inheritance file;
  • title documents and proof of the marital share;
  • real-estate register extracts, bank information, vehicle records and proof of rights in companies;
  • valuation reports for disputed assets;
  • evidence of the heir under the will using the apartment or other property if a reduction of the compulsory share is sought.

Steps before the notary and in court

  1. Apply to the notary to accept the inheritance within the six-month period.
  2. Raise the right to a compulsory share and submit supporting documents.
  3. Obtain information about the assets, the will and other heirs. Arrange a valuation if necessary.
  4. Check the statutory and compulsory share calculations, the marital share and the assets not disposed of by the will.
  5. If there is a dispute, identify the relevant claim. It may involve recognition of a right, inclusion of an asset in the estate, redetermination of shares, partial invalidation of an inheritance certificate or reduction of the compulsory share.
  6. After the judgment, complete the formalities with the notary and register real estate rights.

The appropriate claim depends on whether certificates have been issued, whether a transfer of title has been registered and what property has already passed to other heirs. A claim for the wrong remedy can leave the dispute unresolved even if the heir’s eligibility is established.

Common mistakes

  • Taking half the apartment without first calculating the statutory share.
  • Failing to separate the marital share from the estate.
  • Counting only property left under the will and overlooking deposits, vehicles and other property.
  • Leaving out heirs who would have inherited under the law.
  • Missing the six-month deadline despite qualifying for a compulsory share.
  • Contesting the will when a properly made compulsory-share claim would suffice.
  • Seeking denial solely because the heir under the will lives in the disputed property, without assessing the other conditions of Article 1149(4).

Frequently asked questions

Can a pensioner receive a compulsory share if everything was left to someone else?

The age criterion can establish eligibility for a person in a statutory category, such as a spouse, parent or child of the deceased. The thresholds are 55 for women and 60 for men. The heir must still accept the inheritance. The calculation accounts for the whole estate and all statutory heirs.

Can a minor renounce their compulsory share?

Renouncing an inheritance on a minor’s behalf involves safeguards for the child’s property rights, including prior permission from the guardianship authority in cases prescribed by law. Assess this before filing with the notary. Renunciation cannot freely be withdrawn later.

Does a lifetime gift reduce the compulsory share?

An asset genuinely given away during the deceased’s lifetime and no longer owned by them at death does not ordinarily form part of the estate. Check the gift’s validity, when title passed and whether the apparent gift concealed a different transaction.

Can the compulsory share be paid in cash instead of an interest in an apartment?

That depends on the estate, any agreement between heirs and the facts of the dispute. The fact that an asset cannot be divided does not automatically trigger compensation. The legal grounds, amount and arrangements for payment require an individual assessment.
