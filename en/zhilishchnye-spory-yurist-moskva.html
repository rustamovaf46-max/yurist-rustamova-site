Housing litigation in Moscow

Fatima Rustamova represents clients in housing disputes before Russian courts in Moscow and the Moscow Region. The correct claim depends on ownership, occupancy rights, family relationships, registration documents, contracts and the procedural stage.

Right to occupy and use an apartment

Disputes may concern moving into a dwelling, obstacles to using it, determining arrangements between co-owners or recognising that a person has lost the right to use it. Residence registration alone does not conclusively establish or extinguish property rights. The Housing Code, Civil Code and relevant facts must be examined together.

Eviction and former family members

Eviction is subject to legal grounds and procedural safeguards. The outcome can depend on ownership, tenancy status, family relationships, prior agreements and circumstances protected by law. An owner should not assume that an unwanted occupant can simply be removed without the applicable procedure.

Utility bills and management companies

Disputes can involve incorrect charges, separate payment documents, recalculation, services not provided and responsibilities of a management organisation. Useful evidence includes bills, meter readings, correspondence, payment receipts and inspection reports. The applicable rules depend on the service and the legal relationship.

Flooding and damage to an apartment

Claims after water damage require evidence of the incident, the source of leakage, the responsible party, causation and the amount of loss. Inspection records, photographs, repair estimates and expert findings may matter. Liability cannot be assigned to a neighbour or management company without examining the cause.

Documents and evidence

Collect EGRN extracts, title or tenancy documents, residence and family records where relevant, bills, receipts, correspondence, inspection reports and existing court decisions. For time-sensitive cases, state the date of the next hearing or appeal deadline.

Court process and costs

State duties, experts and other external costs may be additional. The final scope is agreed in advance; no outcome is guaranteed.

Choosing the appropriate remedy

Before filing, distinguish ownership disputes from occupancy rights and housing obligations. For denied access, examine title, occupancy documents, changed locks, correspondence, police reports and witness evidence. A fractional ownership interest does not by itself determine the appropriate remedy or actual arrangements for using the apartment.

Article 31 of the Russian Housing Code contains qualifications: a former family member may retain occupancy rights in certain circumstances. Establish whether the dwelling is privately owned, held under a social tenancy or subject to another special regime. A claim for eviction is not interchangeable with a claim recognising loss of occupancy rights. Identify the proper parties and distinguish residence registration from substantive rights.

Under shared ownership, the court considers existing usage arrangements, relative shares, room layout, practical usability and other occupants' interests. Determining use is not the same as partition or allocation of a share in kind; claims and expert evidence may differ.

Alterations and technical evidence

Unauthorised alterations require examination of technical records, approvals and safety. Water damage disputes may require a court-appointed expert to establish the source, boundary of responsibility and repair costs. Obtain floor plans, technical inventory records, inspection reports, photographs, estimates and evidence of prior demands, possession and expenses. A generic pleading cannot replace the relevant inspection and legal analysis.

Legal sources and related reading

Relevant sources include Articles 30, 31 and 69 of the Russian Housing Code, Articles 209 and 247 of the Civil Code and Resolution No. 14 of the Plenum of the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation dated 2 July 2009, as amended. The applicable version and legally relevant date must be checked against the facts. Library articles are not represented as judgments in the author's own cases.
